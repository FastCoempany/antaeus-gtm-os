Three sentences is plenty.

We do not need a brief, a deck, or a call. We need to know what the thing is and who it is for.

01

You send the idea.

A paragraph. A link to something you like. A photo of a sketch on a napkin. Whatever you actually have.

02

We build it.

A real page, in a browser, with your words and your colours. If you have logos or photographs, we use them. If you do not, we draw.

03

You change one thing.

One round is included. Tell us what is wrong and we fix it, then you get the file and the link.

{{PRICE}}

Flat. Paid up front. No hourly rate, no scoping call, no proposal to sign.

Five days

From the day you send it. Most land sooner; none land later without us telling you first.

One round

What you get.

What you get

  • One HTML file that opens in any browser, and a link you can send.
  • A page that works on a phone, not just on the laptop we built it on.
  • Your real words and your real photographs where you have them.
  • The source, so you or your developer can pick it up and keep going.

What it isn't

  • A working product. Buttons look right; nothing is wired to a database.
  • A brand identity. We work in the one you have, or a plain one.
  • A logo design, a naming exercise, or a strategy document.
  • A long engagement. It is one thing, once, for a flat price.

Questions.

Is the work above real?
The five brands are real and the material is theirs — the renders, the photography, the technical drawings, the palettes and the words all come from their own files. The pages themselves are ours, built to show what the material can do. The prices and stock counts inside them are ours, because none of these shops exists yet. The product names are theirs.
What if I do not have a logo or photographs?
Then we draw. Four of the five pages above lean on assets the brand already owned; the parts nobody had, we built.
Can you copy a site I like?
We will happily take its structure — where the price sits, how the scroll is paced. We will not copy its look, and neither should you.
What do you need from me?
What the thing is, who it is for, and anything you already have. If that is three sentences and a photo, that is enough to start.
What happens after the five days?
You own it. Take it to a developer, take it to an investor, or take it to the next person who asks what you are building.
