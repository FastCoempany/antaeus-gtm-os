# DARKEST SHADES — build reference

Everything below is read off the real material. Every asset is cited by filename only. Where I give a hex, a ratio or a millimetre figure, it was measured off the file, not estimated — I've flagged the few places where I'm inferring.

**Scope note:** the folder is a shared vendor workspace containing other companies' brand work. I studied only Darkest Shades. Other brands found in there are excluded by instruction, including the one that answers your temple-wordmark question (see §8).

## 1. THE PRODUCTS

### What it actually is
A direct-to-consumer premium sunglasses line. Nine frame models, designed in CAD, **physically sampled** (real acetate prototypes exist — photographed in `IMG_2691.jpeg`, `IMG_2692.jpeg`, `IMG_2693.jpeg`, and a pan video `IMG_2698.mov`). Not vapourware, not renders-only.

Construction, in the founder's own words in a vendor thread: *"Its acetate and metal."*

### Stated product specification (`DARKEST SHADES BRAND STORY V1.pdf`)
- **Frames:** premium acetate — "lightweight, ultra-durable, and rich with texture"
- **Lenses:** **TAC (Tri-Acetate Cellulose)**, **ultra-dark polarized**, impact + scratch resistant, **100% UVA/UVB**
- **Fit:** "universal fit" — one nominal fit across styles, explicitly a design constraint
- **Price:** **$100 direct-to-consumer**, "no middlemen"
- **Service:** 45-day no-questions exchange; 6-month warranty
- The hero attribute is the blackout lens — *"a signature blackout effect that makes a statement without saying a word"*

### Hinge / bridge / temple mechanics
- Two hinge systems: a **visible barrel hinge** (silver rivets, knurled cylinder — clearest in Model 08 and in the physical-sample photo `IMG_2104.jpeg`) and a **buried wire hinge** where a flat-oval metal temple exits a wedge-shaped acetate endpiece.
- Metal temples are a **flattened-oval section ~3 mm**, blackened, running from a hard-bevelled acetate endpiece to an acetate temple tip. Where the metal enters the tip there is a **red + electric-blue stripe inlay** (visible on Model 02).
- Several models carry an **electric-blue accent tip** on the temple end and a small blue tab at the endpiece (Models 04, 05).
- Nose bridges are cut straight into the acetate — no separate pads on the acetate models. Models 01/07/08 use a raised brow bar over a cut-out bridge.

### Packaging
A **matte-black rigid two-part box**, wider than deep, with a subtle linen/ribbed texture. Face: the tagline in white caps top-left; a **band of the white chevron pattern** running across the lower third. Long side: body copy in small white sans. Short side / end: mark + stacked wordmark. Renders in `Darkest Shades Brand Guidelines.pdf` p43 show them stacked like shoeboxes, and there's a **thin warm-red/orange line visible along the box's edge seam** — an inner-tray colour flash.

### Temple branding (the production answer)
`IMG_2104.jpeg` is a close-up of an actual manufactured temple: on gloss-black acetate, the **brand mark rendered as a fine cross-hatch/engraved texture**, followed by the **stacked two-line "DARKEST SHADES" wordmark** — i.e. the *secondary* lockup, placed on the **outer** face of the temple near the hinge, reading front-to-back. Silver hinge hardware immediately behind it.

## 2. MATERIALS AND FINISHES AT BUILD FIDELITY

All hexes sampled from the render pixels.

**Studio backdrop / ground.** One infinite seamless sweep, flat **#D2D2D2** (occasionally #D3D3D3). No horizon line. The floor darkens only to **#C3C3C3–#C8C8C8** under the object. This is a ~82% neutral grey — not white. Getting this right is half the look.

**Shadow.** Measured: a broad soft pool starting at the contact line, spreading ~5–10% *wider* than the object, bottoming out at **#C0** — only **~8–10% darker than the backdrop** — and gone within about one-eighth of the object height. In three-quarter views it stretches into two long, very soft parallel streaks (one per temple). This is a huge-softbox / HDRI-dome setup. **Never a hard drop shadow.** In CSS terms: a very wide, very low-opacity elliptical blur, `rgba(0,0,0,0.06–0.10)`, blur radius roughly equal to the object's height.

**Gloss black acetate.** Base **#131313–#1B1B1B**, mid-tones #2E2E2E–#383838. Behaves like polished dark resin: near-mirror on the flat facets, so it mostly reads as *reflections of the grey room* rather than as black. Every chamfer carries a **hard, thin, near-white specular line (#C8–#E8)** about 1–3 px at render scale. Sub-surface is real — the acetate goes semi-translucent at thin edges and around the lens groove, lifting to #3A–#50 there.

**Satin/matte black acetate** (temple tips, Model 05 body). Base **#202020–#262626**, broad diffuse falloff, highlight tops out around #6A–#80 and is wide and soft rather than a line.

**Blue tortoise acetate** (Models 01, 07). Near-black ground **#131313–#1A273A** with **cobalt-periwinkle flecks #5B8CCF–#6495D0** and deeper navy **#182535**, distributed as irregular organic blobs 3–8% of the frame width, not a repeating pattern. It's *translucent* — light passes through the rim and the flecks glow from behind (edge samples reach **#93B5BF–#A7BEC6**). Build it as a mottled noise layer with a `screen`/`lighten` pass and a strong edge-glow.

**Red tortoise acetate** (Model 02). Near-black **#2E2E2E** ground, **oxblood flecks #4B1313–#501414**, plus warm-grey marbling. Same translucency behaviour; the red reads hottest where the material is thinnest (rims, endpiece bevel), pushing toward pure crimson.

**Translucent smoke acetate** (Model 08). **#676767–#999A9E** with visible depth — you can see the far rim through the near one. Hard chamfers everywhere, each catching a bright specular edge.

**Blackened metal temple.** Satin, not chrome. Flat-oval section. Base **#1A1A1A–#2A2A2A** with a single soft anisotropic highlight running the length. Reads dark against the light grey backdrop — it's a *drawn line* in the composition, not a shiny object.

**Brushed metal inlay** (Model 07 temple plate). Pale warm silver, roughly **#B8B8B4–#D0D0CC**, with fine horizontal directional grain, sharp rectangular border, and a **dark line-drawn floral engraving** (cherry blossom, ~5 blooms with leaves) sitting in it at about 1px stroke weight.

**Electric-blue accent.** **#0D12CC–#1F31C8** — a pure, almost ultramarine blue. Tiny area only: a temple-tip cap and a hinge tab. It's the single most saturated thing in the entire product system.

### The lenses — what they actually do
This is the most important material to get right, because "ultra-dark" is the product.

- **Flat blackout lens** (Models 02, 04, 05, 09): sits at **#151515–#2A2A2A**. You cannot see through it at all. What makes it legible is a single broad, soft **sheen band** — a wide diagonal wipe from lower-left to upper-right at roughly **#4A–#6A**, about 25–35% of the lens width, with very soft edges. Plus one small hard specular dot near the top edge. That's it. Two elements: near-black fill + one soft diagonal sheen.
- **Graduated smoke** (Model 08): a clean vertical ramp, **#353535 at the top → #878A8E at the bottom** (dark-over-light — the opposite of a classic gradient lens). Slightly cool/neutral, no colour cast.
- **Silver mirror** (Model 06 shield): behaves as a genuine mirror. It *reflects the grey room*, so it renders as a smooth vertical gradient roughly **#C8 at the top → #9A at the bottom**, with a single long pale streak sweeping across it, and a **1px dark bezel line** tracing the lens perimeter just inside the frame. Mirrored lenses in this system do **not** show a scene — they show a soft, near-featureless environment wash. If you render a reflected skyline in one, it's off-brand.
- **Teal** (Models 01, 07): body **#5BA7BC–#62ABBE**, specular sweep lifting to **#9CC4CE**, darkest at **#489AAF**. Saturated aqua, semi-transparent — you can see the inside of the far temple through it.
- **Amber/yellow** (Model 03): core **#CDAB16**, highlight **#D7BE59**. Warm mustard, more transparent than the others.

Across every lens there is a faint **inner shadow where the lens meets the groove** — a 2–4 px dark ring — and the lens surface is slightly *proud* of the frame front on the acetate models.

## 3. MARKS AND GEOMETRY

### The brand mark — real construction
The mark is an **angular folded polygon**, described in the guidelines as the shape that also drives every graphic element. I traced it directly off `Brand Mark Black.png` and verified the trace at **99.78% pixel agreement** with the original.

**Aspect ratio: 1.9498 : 1** (measured 1788 × 917 px).

It is a **10-vertex closed polygon**, all straight edges. Normalised to a 100 × 100 box (x right, y down):

```
(  0.06,  0.11 )   top-left
( 27.01,  0.11 )   top edge ends
( 54.36, 12.65 )   ← downward V-notch in the TOP edge
( 73.21,  0.11 )   back up to the top line
( 99.94, 41.98 )   right point
( 99.94, 99.89 )   bottom-right
( 73.15, 99.89 )
( 45.41, 87.24 )   ← upward V-notch in the BOTTOM edge
( 26.85, 99.89 )
(  0.06, 58.02 )   back up the left vertical
```

As an SVG path on `viewBox="0 0 1000 513"`:
```
M0,0 L270,0 L544,65 L732,0 L1000,215 L1000,513 L732,513 L454,448 L269,513 L0,298 Z
```

The two notches are the whole idea — a **downward fold at 54% of the width on the top edge and an upward fold at 45% on the bottom edge**, offset from each other, which is what makes it read as a folded/creased banner or a pair of shades seen edge-on. The left edge is vertical for the top 58%; the right edge is vertical for the bottom 58%. It has **180° rotational near-symmetry but is not actually symmetric** — the offset notches are deliberate.

### Lockups — measured ratios

| Asset | Ink dimensions (px) | Aspect |
|---|---|---|
| Brand mark alone | 1788 × 917 | **1.950 : 1** |
| **Primary logo** (mark + one-line wordmark) | 4305 × 294 | **14.643 : 1** |
| Primary wordmark alone | 3572 × 245 | 14.580 : 1 |
| **Secondary logo** (mark + stacked two-line wordmark) | 3579 × 677 | **5.287 : 1** |
| Secondary wordmark alone | 2117 × 661 | 3.203 : 1 |

**Primary lockup internals:** mark height = **1.20 × cap height**; gap between mark and wordmark = **0.28 × mark width** (≈ 0.69 × cap height); wordmark width = **6.24 × mark width**. The mark is vertically centred on the wordmark and over-hangs it top and bottom.

**Secondary lockup internals:** mark height ≈ **1.02 × the height of the two-line word block** (they align top and bottom); gap = **0.108 × mark width**; the two lines have cap height 282 with a 97 px gap → **line pitch = 1.34 × cap height**; both lines flush left.

### Clear space — the stated rule
From the SAFEZONES page of `Darkest Shades Brand Guidelines.pdf`:

> "The height of the safe zone (X) is determined by the logo mark part width. This measurement is utilized to establish a safe zone around the entire logo. The identical guidelines are applicable to brand mark and secondary logo versions."
>
> **X = [mark] width.**

So: **clear space on all four sides = 1 × the width of the brand mark** (not its height). That is a *very* generous margin — roughly 0.13 × the primary lockup's own width on each side. The diagram shows ghosted copies of the mark butted against all four edges of the lockup's bounding box.

### The three graphic-element systems
The guidelines ship three, all derived from the mark:

**Element 1 — "Massive, large graphic element based on the brandmark."** A single enlarged angular shape, used as (a) a hairline outline, (b) a solid fill, (c) a **photo mask**. Also shown quartered — the shape cut into four corner fragments deployed around a layout.

**Element 2 — "More minimalistic and lighter version of the lines based on the shape of the brand name."** Open hairline polygons, two shapes crossing at a single point to make a bow-tie/hourglass crossing. Three variants (01/02/03). Stroke weight is 1px-equivalent and uniform. The **PATTERN** page shows four of these interlocked, edges crossing, forming a loose asymmetric lattice.

**Element 3 — "Based on the geometric shapes of the brand mark and can be transformed into a pattern or a chain of elements."** Solid five- and six-sided chevron/kite forms that **touch at single points** and chain horizontally into a repeating zig-zag band. Two chain variants (02 tighter, 03 looser). **This is the one that ends up on the packaging and the posters** — a white chevron band across a black field.

### The Eclipse Silhouette emblem — a separate, later thing
`brand/emblems/eclipse-silhouette/` holds an entirely **different** mark: a soft, asymmetric, cloud-or-wave outline with a continuous interior opening, drawn as a variable-width ribbon. Vector at viewBox `28 234 192 88` → **2.182 : 1**.

Its reference image `supplied-render.png` is captioned **"DIRECTION 2 / ECLIPSE SILHOUETTE"** and shows it as a **polished raised gold metal emblem applied to the temple of a black/tortoise acetate frame** — i.e. a hardware/jewellery direction, not a logo.

Treatments: black `#000000`, white `#FFFFFF`, flat gold **`#C9AA78`**, and a gold gradient. The gradient, verbatim from `DS-Eclipse-gold-gradient.svg` — a *near-vertical* linear gradient (`x1=0 y1=0 x2=0.18 y2=1`) alternating dark and pale to fake metal:

```
offset 0     #8B6635
offset 0.16  #F7E7C5
offset 0.36  #C7A575
offset 0.53  #F6E4BD
offset 0.72  #A47A43
offset 0.87  #DEC399
offset 1     #76552D
```

That seven-stop alternating ladder is a genuinely reusable ingredient — it's the cleanest "polished gold" gradient in the whole archive.

**Authority caveat, stated by the files themselves.** `START-HERE.txt` and `manifest.json` say plainly: *"These are newly reconstructed files, not recovered originals… Its small size, perspective, lighting and bevels mean an exact original outline cannot be established."* And: *"Gold is an approximate screen color inferred from the render, not a verified brand color."* And: *"The existing brand guide's angular mark was not substituted for this emblem."*

**What that means for you:** the Eclipse contour is a good-faith redraw off a low-res screenshot — treat its curve as *directionally right, not authoritative*. The gold is a screen approximation, not a specified ink or foil. And crucially, the emblem does **not** replace the angular mark; the two coexist as separate objects at different points in time. The angular mark carries the real authority (approved, source-file'd, manufactured onto product). The Eclipse is an exploration.

> "What I'd like to do is stray away from vibrant colors. I want to focus the options on the name. 'Darkest'"

That last line is the colour system in one sentence.

### Typography — the real names
`Darkest Shades Brand Guidelines.pdf`, FONTS page:

> "Darkest Shades uses **Owners Wide** and **Stolzl** typography system. Use as shown on the right to preserve consistency across all materials."

- **Primary — Owners Wide.** Titles only. A very wide, heavy neo-grotesque with flat terminals and tight apertures. Set in **all caps**, tight tracking, tight leading (roughly 0.95–1.0× the cap height between baselines — lines nearly touch). This is the voice of the brand.
- **Secondary — Stolzl.** Everything else: body, labels, nav, captions. A clean geometric sans with rounded bowls and a single-storey feel. The rationale, verbatim from the thread: *"The Primary Font (Owners Wide) is slightly stretched in width, which perfectly complements our logo since the logo also has a slightly elongated feel… The Secondary Font (Stolzl) is clean, minimalistic, and has rounded letterforms that pair beautifully with the primary font."*
- The rejected pairing was **New Science + Tablet Gothic Wide** (Option 02 in `Darkest Shades FONT PAIRS.pdf`).

**Type-scale rules as actually practised** (measured off the decks, since no numeric scale is published):
- One giant headline per surface, in caps, Owners Wide, ragged-right, 3–5 words a line, leading set so ascenders nearly kiss descenders.
- A **mono-ish micro-kicker above the headline** in Stolzl, all caps, ~1/8 the headline size, **letterspaced roughly +0.12 em** — `PURPOSE`, `POSITIONING STATEMENT`, `BRAND STRATEGY`.
- Body at roughly 1/6 the headline size, generous leading (~1.35), set in **narrow columns of 2–4 lines**, often two or three columns side by side.
- Running head top-left in ~11px Stolzl (`Brand Identity / Logotype`) with a **hairline rule running from just after it to the right margin**.
- Lead-in bolding inside body copy: the first two or three words set in the bold weight (`**Our shades** are more than just eyewear…`).
- Small labels are set in **pill outlines** — 1px stroke, fully rounded ends (`Title`, `Body`, `Authenticity`, `Discretion`).

### Grid and layout (measured off 1920 × 1080 pages)
- **Outer margin: 26 px** on a 1920-wide page (1.33%).
- **Running-head hairline at y = 34 px** (3.15% down); the kicker sits on that baseline.
- **The identity-page split is at x = 499 px = 25.97% of the width.** Left 26% = white text column; right 74% = `#F1F2F1` panel or full-bleed image. That's effectively a **3 : 9 split on a 12-column grid**. Every logo/type/graphic-element page uses it.
- Image-led pages abandon the split and go full-bleed edge to edge, with type overlaid centred.
- The render deck uses a different, simpler grid: white page, one **#D2D2D2 tile inset 35 px on all sides**, model name top-right in white bold caps, page number bottom-right in dark.

## 5. THE VISUAL LANGUAGE

### How product is rendered (the studio language)
- **Backdrop:** infinite seamless `#D2D2D2`. No horizon, no gradient, no vignette, no reflective floor.
- **Lighting:** one very large soft key plus a broad ambient dome. The key sits **upper-front, and swings left or right per shot** — I measured it upper-right in one front view and upper-left in the shield three-quarter. There is **no rim light and no hard key**.
- **Shadow:** as measured in §2 — max ~8–10% darker than backdrop, wider than the object, gone within an eighth of the object's height, resolving into two long soft streaks in three-quarter views.
- **Camera:** long lens, minimal perspective distortion, object occupying **55–70% of the frame width**, centred, slightly **above** the object's centreline looking gently down. Four canonical views per model: **front · profile · three-quarter-front · three-quarter-rear**.
- **Colour grade:** none. Straight neutral. The grey stays grey.
- **Crop:** always full-object with air. The deck never crops into the product — but the founder explicitly asked for that in the catalogue brief, saying he wanted *"prismatic logo elements and patterns in shadows, lighting, background."* That instruction — **the mark's angular shapes cast as shadows or projected as light across a product shot** — is the intended next step and is unbuilt. It's the single best unexploited idea in the archive.

### How people are photographed (the campaign language)
Completely different register from the product shots. From the guidelines' imagery and `Moodboard_Darkest Shades.pdf`:
- **Flash-lit, grainy, night-coded.** Direct on-camera flash, hard falloff into black, visible grain, the look of a paparazzi frame or a 2000s point-and-shoot.
- **Tight crops on the face**, often cropped through the forehead or chin. The eyes are never visible — the lens is the subject.
- Colour grade warm-to-neutral with **crushed blacks**; backgrounds go to true black, not grey.
- Subjects are **mid-motion or mid-glance**, never posed-smiling. Confidence, not charm.
- A recurring device: **one bright specular star on the lens** where the flash hits.
- Group shots are frontal and symmetrical, three abreast.

The moodboard the founder chose points at: dark editorial fashion layout, big white type over black, matte black packaging on concrete, a woman half-submerged in a pool wearing wraparound shades, black-clad figures under hard light, minimal web layouts with huge display type — plus the three swatches black/grey/tan. It is pointing at **quiet luxury photographed like nightlife**.

### Layout devices, concretely
1. **Mark-as-mask** — a portrait clipped inside the angular polygon.
2. **Mark-as-outline** — a hairline version of the shape drawn *around* a portrait, with the headline sitting inside the outline's lower half.
3. **Mark-as-speech-shape** — a solid white angular field on black holding the headline and a short body column (the "SHADE YOUR EYES" poster).
4. **Chevron band** — the Element-3 chain running full-width as a divider between the type block and the image (posters and packaging).
5. **Hairline lattice** — the Element-2 open polygons as a large faint white line drawing over black, with body copy sitting inside one of the cells.
6. **Corner logo, opposite-corner label** — mark top-left, small caps kicker bottom-left or bottom-right. The mark is never centred in a campaign layout.

## 7. THE STRONGEST INGREDIENTS

The twelve things I'd actually build with.

1. **The ten-vertex angular mark, exact.** You have the verified path. Its power is that it's *one shape* that works at four scales: identity mark, photo mask, hairline outline, and chained pattern unit. Nothing else in the system does that much work.

2. **The double-notch.** The downward fold at 54% top / upward fold at 45% bottom. It is the brand's only piece of true geometric signature, and it survives at any scale, in outline or solid, chained or alone. Build it as a reusable clip-path.

3. **`#D2D2D2` backdrop + an 8%-opacity shadow.** The studio language in two values. It's counter-intuitive — most product pages go white — and the flat mid-grey is exactly why these renders read expensive. Copy it literally.

4. **The blackout lens recipe:** `#151515` fill + one soft diagonal sheen band at `#4A–#6A` across 30% of the lens + one small hard specular dot. That's the hero product attribute reduced to two CSS gradients.

5. **`#000000` + `#FFFFFF` + `#BF835E` held in reserve.** The strongest colour pair here is black-and-white with a single terracotta note that the brand has barely spent. Use it once per composition and it will look authored rather than default.

6. **The chevron chain band.** Element 3 chained into a repeating zig-zag: solid white kites touching at single points on a black field. It's the one pattern that already exists on real packaging and real posters, and it tiles trivially.

7. **The `#0D12CC` electric blue as a one-pixel product accent.** The single most saturated element in the entire brand, appearing only as a temple tip. Rationed exactly the way orange is rationed in a good system.

8. **Owners Wide caps, five words, ragged right, leading at ~0.95.** The headline lockup is the brand's loudest asset. `NOT JUST / SUNGLASSES. / A STATEMENT.` — three lines, three sizes of nothing else on the surface.

9. **The mono kicker + hairline rule.** `PURPOSE` in +0.12em letterspaced caps at 11px, with a hairline running to the right margin. It's the cheapest possible way to make any surface read as part of this system.

10. **The mark-as-mask portrait.** A face clipped inside the angular polygon on a black field, with the mark's two notches slicing through the image. It's in the guidelines, it's in the graphic-elements deck, and it's the most distinctive composition the brand owns.

11. **The seven-stop gold gradient** from `DS-Eclipse-gold-gradient.svg`. A genuinely good faked-metal ramp — alternating `#8B6635 / #F7E7C5 / #C7A575 / #F6E4BD / #A47A43 / #DEC399 / #76552D` on a near-vertical axis. Drop it on any solid shape and you get convincing polished metal in one declaration.

12. **"See Everything. Reveal Nothing."** paired with a lens that shows nothing. The tagline and the product make the same argument, which is rare. If you build one composition, build that one.

*Two more worth a line:* the **translucent tortoise acetate** (near-black ground, cobalt or oxblood flecks, edge-glow) is a much richer surface than flat black and is under-used in the deck. And **the 26% / 74% page split** is a small, specific layout rule that will make anything you build sit inside the system immediately.

## 8. CONTRADICTIONS AND CAUTIONS

**The temple wordmark you saw is not Darkest Shades.** I chased this down. Three image files in the CAD/render vendor folder (`image_7.png`, `image_10.png`, `image_11.png`) are **advertising creative belonging to a third-party eyewear company**, supplied by the founder to a 3D artist purely as *rendering-style references* — "here's the lighting and finish I want." Those ads carry that company's wordmark on the temple and its own model names. They are not Darkest Shades product, were never intended as such, and their brand name is a near-homophone of what you read, which is almost certainly the source of the confusion.

**Darkest Shades is current, and the real temple branding is documented.** `IMG_2104.jpeg` is a photograph of an actual manufactured Darkest Shades temple carrying the angular mark in engraved cross-hatch plus the stacked two-line `DARKEST SHADES` wordmark. The video `IMG_2698.mov` (Jan 2025) shows the same branding across multiple manufactured frames. Note the sequencing: **the nine renders were made in Aug 2024, before the logo was approved (Oct 2024), so every model in `Renders_Sunglasses_presentation.pdf` is completely unbranded.** If you want a branded render, you have to add the mark yourself — and the temple, near the hinge, outer face, is where it goes.

### Other things that don't line up

**Two different "marks" coexist.** The 2025 guidelines mark is a hard-edged angular polygon. The Eclipse Silhouette (Sept 2026) is a soft curved ribbon. They share no geometry, no construction logic and no era. The emblem's own notes say the angular mark "was not substituted for this emblem" — so it's an *addition*, labelled "DIRECTION 2", not a replacement. Treat the angular mark as canonical; treat Eclipse as a live hardware exploration whose contour is a redraw from a screenshot and whose gold is an approximated screen colour, not a specified foil.

**Render deck mislabels Model 09 as Model 08.** Pages 36–38 of `Renders_Sunglasses_presentation.pdf` are headed `MODEL 08` but show the chunky flared-tab frame that the index identifies as **Model 09**. Eight pages are labelled Model 08; only five are actually Model 08.

**Two errors on the Values page.** The INDIVIDUALITY row's three chips read *Authenticity · Individuality · **Individuality*** — the third is a duplicate, one value is missing. And **CULTURAL CONNECTION's body text is a verbatim copy of PRIVACY's** ("Maintain your personal space and mystery, even in public."). Neither is intentional; don't propagate them.

**No colour palette page exists in the brand guidelines.** The palette lives only in the moodboard deck as three swatch circles. `#BF835E` is a *moodboard* swatch, never applied anywhere in the identity executions. If you use it, you're extending the system, not following it.

**The logotype's typeface is an open question the founder raised and never got answered.** On 15 Sep 2025 he asked directly: *"standing by for the links/licenses to the fonts. also can you confirm that this logo font is either the stolzl font or the owners wide font?"* The contract ended without a reply. Visually the wordmark is a wide neo-grotesque consistent with the Owners family, but it is set **noticeably lighter and much more loosely tracked** than the `OWNERS WIDE` specimen on the same page. **Don't assume the wordmark is simply Owners Wide typed out** — it may be a modified or different cut. For recreation, use the vector from `Primary Logo Black.png` / the `.ai` files rather than re-typesetting it.

**No font licences are on record.** Owners Wide and Stolzl are commercial retail faces. Nothing in the archive shows a licence, a foundry, or a webfont entitlement. For HTML/CSS work you'll need to license them or substitute — the closest free approximations are a wide grotesque (Archivo Expanded, or Anton for display weight) for Owners Wide, and a geometric sans (Poppins, Hanken Grotesk) for Stolzl.

**The render brief was never executed.** The founder scoped a photorealistic catalogue — *"incorporating those prismatic logo elements and patterns in shadows, lighting, background"* — quoted at $1,200–$1,600, and it was deferred. So the 39-page deck is **engineering-grade CAD visualisation, not brand photography**. It's flat, neutral, unlit-for-drama, and unbranded. Everything you see in it is accurate to the object and *silent* about the brand. The brand-photographic language for product doesn't exist yet — which means it's yours to invent, and the founder already told you what he wanted it to be.

**Work-in-progress / rejected directions to avoid.** `Darkest Shades Logo 2.pdf` and `Darkest Shades Logotypes.pdf` contain five *rejected* brand-mark directions: a lens-aperture monogram, a chevron/V, a diagonal double-stripe, a four-petal square, and several wordmark treatments (mixed-weight `DARkEST SHADEs`, lowercase `darkest shades`, an `EST 2024 / EYEWEAR` badge). Numbered jpgs `13.jpg`–`25.jpg` are the same round. **None of these are the brand.** Likewise `H01.png / H03.png / H04.png` and `J03.png / J05.png` are the founder's own pre-brand 3D sketches from Jul 2024 — earlier than, and superseded by, the nine-model CAD set.

**Finally: the deck's own competitor/opportunity map places the brand at "high-end × minimal design."** That quadrant, not the maximal or high-street ones, is the target. Anything you build that reads busy or trend-chasing is aiming at the wrong corner of the brand's own map.
