## 1. The products

This is a **founder-led menswear capsule**, not a shipped range. Exactly one style reached a real physical prototype; three others stalled at sourcing/moodboard. Everything below is real, documented product truth.

### 1.2 The pattern — what the DXF actually contains

`AC-LST-001.dxf` and `AC-LST-001_2.dxf` are **ASTM D6673 apparel pattern exports from Gerber AccuMark 17.0.0**. Not drawings — real graded production patterns.

- **28 BLOCKs = 7 pieces × 4 sizes (S/M/L/XL).** Piece names: `_FR` (front), `_BK` (back), `_BK YOKE`, `_SLV` (sleeve), `_SLV BK` (sleeve back), `_NB` (neckband), `_CUFF`.
- Entities: 852 POLYLINE / 3,751 VERTEX (piece outlines and internal lines), 366 POINT (drill/notch marks), 353 TEXT, 124 LINE, 48 ATTDEF, 28 INSERT.
- Layers (Gerber convention): `1` = cut boundary, `8` = grainline, `14` = internal lines, `2`/`3`/`5`/`6`/`7` = notches and marks, `80`/`84`/`85`/`87` = annotation.
- Embedded per-piece TEXT: `Piece Name:`, `SIZE:`, `CATEGORY:` (BACK / FRONT / SLEEVE / SLEEVE BACK / CUFF / NECKBAND / BACK YOKE), `Material: A` or `Material: B`, `Quantity: 1,0`, `ANNOTATION: 1 X FABRIC`, `Link:4`. **Material A/B assignment matches the tech-pack BOM exactly** — body pieces A, neckband + cuff B.
- `Style Name: AC-LST-001`, `Creation Date: 23-09-2024`, `Creation Time: 21:55`.

**Piece cut-boundary bounding boxes, inches** (layer 1; pieces nested rotated, so read as length × half-width):

| Piece | S | M | L | XL |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FR | 30.52 × 10.38 | 31.51 × 11.13 | 32.51 × 11.88 | 33.50 × 12.88 |
| BK | 24.16 × 10.38 | 24.97 × 11.13 | 25.76 × 11.88 | 26.56 × 12.88 |
| BK YOKE | 7.03 × 8.69 | 7.22 × 9.07 | 7.43 × 9.44 | 7.63 × 9.94 |

| SLV BK | 22.97 × 6.28 | 23.29 × 6.68 | 23.60 × 7.09 | 23.88 × 7.63 |

Two of these reconcile perfectly against the spec and are worth trusting as construction truth:

- **Front half-width 10.38"** = 10" (quarter chest at S) + 3/8" side SA. Confirms **front cut on the fold**.

Matching Gerber-native exports exist as `AC-LST-001.zip` / `AC-LST-001_2.zip`; flat nested plots as `AC-LST-001_Pattern PDF.pdf` (~53 × 84 in, multi-size colored grade lines).

## 2. Materials and finishes at build fidelity

Every hex below is **measured with PIL** (mean RGB over a cropped patch) from the named file, not eyeballed.

### 2.1 The thermal knit — the signature surface

The face is a **micro-waffle / bird's-eye**: a **half-drop (staggered) lattice of tiny raised yarn crossings with a dark pinhole at each cell centre**. Not a big honeycomb waffle — the cells are ~1.2 mm pitch, so at arm's length it reads as a fine matte grain, and only at macro distance resolves into diamonds. The two yarn families cross at roughly ±45°, so a canvas recreation wants two offset diagonal line sets plus a dot grid of shadowed pinholes.

The **reverse is completely different** — smooth, flat, slightly warmer and duller, like a fine jersey. Both faces are visible in the same shot (`Photo May 13 2025, 4 04 56 PM.jpg`), which is the single best texture reference in the archive.

**Light behaviour:** the raised crossings catch a soft specular ridge highlight while the pinholes hold a small hard shadow, so the fabric has high micro-contrast but low macro-contrast. Under raking light the whole plane goes from `#A89F8A` to `#4C493E` across ~15 cm. Cotton, so the sheen is diffuse — no gloss.

### 2.2 The 2×2 rib (neckband and cuffs)

Pairs of raised wales alternating with recessed pairs, ~1.5–2 mm pitch, running **perpendicular to the neckline/cuff edge** and following the curve. 380 gsm — noticeably denser and slightly darker in tone than the body because the ribs self-shadow.

- Rib neckband, warm light (`IMG_4026.jpeg`): `#A59869`
- Rib cuff, indoor (`00000126-…jpg`): `#9C998B`
- Rib cuff (`IMG_4024.jpeg`): `#8F8C78`

### 2.3 The wash — this is the most important finish in the brand

Two things are happening and they're separable.

**(a) The specified wash — "ASUTE GARMENT WASH"**, a potassium-permanganate snow wash. The process is printed verbatim in the tech pack (p8), and it tells you exactly what the surface should look like: permanganate solution absorbed into **white stone or foam balls**, tumbled against the garment in a "snow machine" for 3–10 minutes, then neutralised. *"The humidity is determined according to the required snowflake point size. If it is large, the medium should be wet, and if it is small, it should be dry."* → the visual signature is **irregular pale speckle-clouds of variable grain size**, not uniform fade.

**(b) The wash the founder actually wanted — "seam highlighter wash".** Verbatim, from the manufacturer thread:
> *"The current prototype is missing the seam-fade or high-low wash detail we're aiming for. We are trying to replicate the vintage wash effect shown in the reference garments, where the seams, neckline, shoulders, sleeve cuffs, and hem appear naturally lighter due to intentional fading or abrasion… From my research - this look is commonly referred to as a 'sun-faded wash' or 'seam highlighter wash.'"*

For build purposes: **a lighter-value halo running along every seam, the neckband edge, the shoulder line, the cuff and the hem**, plus broad soft tonal cloudiness across the panels. Visible on the real sample as horizontal streaky banding — `Photo May 13 2025, 3 45 48 PM.jpg` shows pale streaks at `#A08D64` against body `#7E6F4D`. Best reference for the effect done well: `Shirt Blank 1 - closeup.png` (hem fold and seam markedly lighter than the body).

### 2.4 Seams and stitches as drawn surfaces

- **Overlock (ISO #514)** reads as a raised ridge ~3–4 knit cells wide, the seam allowance pressed to one side, with a bright crest and a soft shadow on the far side. The serge itself is a tight chain of fine diagonal loops along the fold. Clearly visible in `Photo May 13 2025, 4 05 10 PM.jpg`.
- **Coverstitch (#605 / #406)** is two or three parallel needle lines with a looper chain on the reverse; at the hem it sits 1/4" apart.
- **A real defect worth knowing:** the physical sample was overlocked but **not topstitched** — the tech pack change log flags it with before/after photos. So the prototype you see in photos is missing the topstitch the spec calls for.
- Cuff topstitching options were studied explicitly in `image_6.png`: *"No topstitching"* / *"Single row of topstitching"* / *"Two rows of topstitching (straddle stitch)"*.

### 2.5 Embroidery — the only real decoration on the product

Spec (`AC-LST-001_Tech Pack_6-15-25.pdf` p15):
- **Thread: PANTONE 19-3943 TXC BELLWETHER BLUE.** Swatch on that page measures **`#24315A`** — a deep navy-indigo.
- **Technique, verbatim: "Satin stitch filled-in lettering" + "zigzag stitching around letter outlines."**
- Size **2.5 in × 1.58 in**, at **center back yoke**.
- The artwork is shown as the `digs` wordmark with a **scalloped/zigzag outline path** drawn around every letter — that bumpy contour is the digitised stitch boundary and is itself a great graphic device.

As actually sewn (`emb.png` crop from `Photo May 13 2025, 3 45 48 PM.jpg`, and `00000126-PHOTO-2025-04-10-03-48-14.jpg`):
- Thread reads **`#3E4351`** in flat studio light, near-black in direct sun (darkest-3% of stitch core reads `#010107` at that exposure).
- The satin stitches lie **perpendicular to each letter stroke**, giving a fine corded/ridged sheen across the stroke width.
- Letter edges are **slightly furry** — stray navy fibres break the outline by a fraction of a millimetre, and the sage knit shows through at the very edge (`#B8A172`).
- Embroidery sits on the back yoke panel, centred, immediately below the neckband.

## 3. Marks and graphics

### 3.1 The `digs` wordmark — real construction

Lowercase, always. Geometric grotesque with **near-perfectly circular bowls**, very tightly tracked so adjacent letters almost kiss.

- **d** — full circle bowl, straight ascender on the right with a flat-cut terminal.
- **i** — short stem with a **detached, perfectly round dot set high, at the same height as the d's ascender top.** This is the mark's signature quirk: in tight tile settings the i reads as an exclamation point, which the brand book leans into — the cover tile literally reads `d!gs`.
- **g** — single-storey, circular bowl, descender that sweeps **down and to the left into an open hook that does not close**.
- **s** — conventional double curve, slightly narrower than the round letters.

Letterforms are consistent with **Alexandria** (the brand typeface) at a weight heavier than the specified Regular. Rendered as outlines in the PDF, not live text.

**Clear space:** *"use the width of the 'S' as a guideline for margins surrounding the logo"* — i.e. one lowercase-s width on all sides, shown on p7 as pale S glyphs at the corners of a 3×3 field.

**Approved wordmark colourways** (p14): white on photography · black on blue · off-white on black · blue on black. Plus, stated as a rule: *"Do place the wordmark at the bottom of the page, edge to edge for graphic impact."*

### 3.2 The `DD` brandmark

A **hand-drawn marker monogram**: two D forms, one normal and one mirrored, overlapped so the bowls intersect and leave a **lens/vesica void in the middle**, with the outer strokes sweeping out to four sharp points — the whole thing reading as a bowtie or a stylised ⋈. Strokes have **ragged, dry-marker edges** with visible bleed and skip; nothing is geometric.

- Clear space: *"use center area of the 'DD' as a guideline for margins."*
- Colourways (p13): white on black · black on ice `#f0f5ff` · blue on white · white on blue.
- It appears in the guide *only* in blue or black or white — never as an accent colour.

### 3.3 The "Actually Dope" scrawl and the primary lockup

**The lockup is the most distinctive graphic in the system**, and the guide says (p8): *"The wordmark and tagline lockup should be used wherever possible, taking priority over the wordmark on it's own."*

Construction: the `digs` wordmark, with **"AcTuAlly DOPE" hand-scrawled in marker straight across it** — set at roughly −8° to −10°, mixed-case, stacked on two lines ("AcTuAlly" over "DOPE") in the tote/cap applications, or running as one skewed line in the flat lockup. The scrawl and the wordmark **overlap and interfere** — that's the point, one reads through the other.

Four approved lockup colourways (p10): white `digs` + blue scrawl on black · black `digs` + blue scrawl on ice · black `digs` + blue scrawl on white · white `digs` + **black** scrawl on blue.

### 3.4 Recurring graphic motifs

1. **The scrawl-over-type device.** Blue or black marker handwriting laid across clean sans type. Used on the wordmark, on headlines (`fashion` scrawled repeatedly over "Designed for those who push boudaries."), and as strikethrough on "Streetwear that echoes your journey."
2. **The scribble layer.** A chaotic field of black marker doodles under/over type — loose strokes, X's, small triangles, hatch and tick marks. Seen on the "Innovative designs for ambitious lives." tile.
3. **Torn black edges.** Ragged black shapes peeking out from behind a blue panel on an ice ground, like torn poster paper.
4. **All-over tag pattern.** "ACTUALLY DOPE" repeated in blue as a wallpaper tile behind/around product — used on the bubble mailer and as a poster border.
5. **The wordmark tile.** `digs` stacked and repeated, white-on-blue and blue-on-white, filling a square edge to edge.
6. **Four-sided marquee frame.** A phrase ("ELEVATE THE DAILY GRIND") set in caps repeating along all four edges of a black frame, rotated 90° on the sides.
7. **Blue border frame.** A solid `#0042e5` rule framing a photograph — used on the billboard and story templates.
8. **The bird / winged-figure chest graphic.** Fine single-weight black line art at centre chest — a flying bird in `Ai1.jpg`, a winged figure in `Ai2.jpg`. **Caveat: these are AI-generated concept mockups with garbled fake wordmarks, explored but never finalised.**

### 4.2 Typography

| Role | Typeface | Weight | Tracking | Case | Application |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Heading | **Alexandria** | Regular | 0 | Title or Sentence | Print/Web |
| Script | **The Old Falcons** | Regular | 0 | Title or Sentence | Print/Web |
| Body | **Alexandria** | Regular | 0 | Title or Sentence | Print/Social |

Embedded font names in the PDF: `Alexandria-Regular` (TrueType) and `TheOldFalconsDemo` (Type 1).

- **Alexandria** is a geometric humanist sans — tall x-height, circular bowls, single-storey `g` with an open descender hook (the same `g` as the logo), flat-ended `t`. It's a Google-Fonts family, freely available, which makes it easy to reproduce.
- **The Old Falcons** is a **dry-marker graffiti hand** — chisel-flat strokes with ragged/skipping edges, wildly mixed case (caps and lowercase mingled inside a single word), variable baseline, a big loose `R` with a kicking leg, tapering terminals. This is what carries all the emotional weight in the system.

**Type rules, verbatim:**
- *"No other typeface should be used for any communication in order to maintain brand salience."*
- *"Style may be left, right or centre-aligned, but never set to full justify."*

**Observed layout rules** (from the applications, not stated but consistent):
- Headlines set very tight — leading around 0.92–0.98× the type size, so lines lock together as a block.
- Caps headlines are set large enough to fill the frame **edge to edge**, with words breaking to fill the measure.
- The script is used for exactly one or two words inside an otherwise-sans sentence — the payoff word ("…to be **dope**.", "wear your **story**", "echoes your **journey**").
- Section dividers: full-bleed blue, giant ice-white numeral ("01", "02") top-right, "Brand Guidelines" set vertically in the script up the right edge beside it, section name large at lower-left.
- Footer blocks are small, right-aligned, stacked: handle / URL / city.

## 5. The visual language

**Product presentation.** There are two completely different modes in the archive and you should know which is which.

*The aspirational mode* (brand book): street photography, desaturated and cool, of models in oversized taupe/sage/stone hoodies and crewnecks with camo cargos, shot against concrete, corrugated shutters, glass office facades, city crossings. Natural light, slightly flat, often near-monochrome so the blue `#0042e5` is the only saturated thing in frame. Crops are tight and off-centre; the subject frequently looks away or down. Interspersed with clean product-on-object shots — a black tee on a black hanger against a plain plaster wall, a woven label on black denim shot macro, a bucket hat worn low.

*The actual mode* (real product): unstyled working documentation. Garment on a **cream canvas size-40 male dress form with a polished aluminium neck cap and a chrome/black stand, against a plain warm-white wall**, natural window light from one side, soft, with real shadows and a vent register in shot. Macro fabric shots taken on a white desk under **hard raking sunlight**, with a fibreglass measuring tape, a mailer, and a laptop in frame. This is honest, un-retouched, and — for building compositions — arguably the more interesting register.

**Poster / billboard system.** A grid of posters wheat-pasted on a hoarding; each poster is one idea in one colour. Billboard: photograph inside a solid blue `#0042e5` border, white headline in Alexandria set flush-left over the top-left of the image, `digs` wordmark bottom-left, and `@streetdigs / www.digs.com / Los Angeles` right-aligned bottom-right.

**Social system.** Two grids, square and story. Recurring layouts:
- Photo + script headline in white + wordmark bottom-centre
- Solid blue square floated on an ice ground, white headline inside, small body paragraph at its foot, torn black shapes behind
- Caps headline filling a black square in blue, or filling a blue square in white
- Repeating-phrase blocks in alternating blue/white bands
- A blue pill bar overlaid on a photo carrying a short line
- A full tile of repeated `digs` wordmarks
- Story frames with a four-sided marquee border, or a hairline blue frame around a portrait, with `digs` bottom-left and a small stacked footer bottom-right

**Tech-pack layout** (worth stealing wholesale — it's a beautiful, rigid system):
- Header: `digs` wordmark top-left in black, a vertical hairline rule, page title centred in caps, season `FW25` top-right in bold
- Sub-header: the style string, pipe-separated — `AC-LST-001 | LONG SLEEVE TEE | SIZE RANGE: S-XL | SAMPLE SIZE: M | STATUS: PROTO | TOPS`
- Body: black-line technical flats on white, with **soft grey diagonal drape strokes** suggesting fabric fall, and **red leader lines with arrowheads** running to caps callouts
- Rib is drawn as a hatch of fine parallel lines; hems as double dashed lines
- Trims are keyed with **small coloured circular tokens** — L1 teal, L2 green, P1 pink, P2 magenta, P3 deep purple
- Dimensions drawn as red arrow-ended measure lines with the value set alongside
- Footer: bold role line left, `UPDATED: 06/15/2025` right, a centred confidentiality line beneath
- Colorways page: two garments per colourway (front + back, overlapping, back tucked behind and to the right), with a swatch-chip legend beneath each

**Moodboard layout** (`digs pant1 (1).pdf`, `Shirts.pdf`): loose photo collage with **yellow sticky-note labels** ("inspiration", "cuff with intentional wrinkle", "slight tail"), **pink sticky notes** for construction paragraphs, **green sticky notes** for hard rules ("draw string, **no belt loops**"), fat **red-orange arrows**, and — on the shirts board — a huge **ragged red brush-marker redaction** covering most of the board with white holes punched through it where notes need to be legible. Crude, urgent, and genuinely striking.

## 7. The strongest ingredients

Ranked by how much striking raw material they give you per unit of effort.

1. **The `digs` / "Actually Dope" lockup.** Clean geometric sans wordmark with a marker scrawl driven straight through it at a slight angle. Two textures, two registers, deliberate collision. Four approved colourways. This is the brand's single best idea and it scales from a 25 mm cap embroidery to a building-sized poster.

2. **The high round dot on the `i`.** Set tight, `digs` reads as `d!gs`. The brand book already exploits it in the tile pattern. It's a free, ownable typographic event — build compositions where the dot becomes a punctuation mark, a bullet, a period, a hole.

3. **The `DD` marker monogram.** A mirrored-D bowtie with a vesica void at its centre and ragged dry-marker edges. Geometrically simple enough to draw in SVG paths, textured enough to be worth rendering properly. It reads at 12 px and at 3 m.

4. **The micro-waffle thermal surface.** A half-drop lattice of raised crossings with dark pinholes at ~1.2 mm pitch, plus a totally different smooth reverse. Two intersecting diagonal line families + a shadowed dot grid gets you 90% of the way in canvas. Measured range `#4C493E` → `#ADA595`. High micro-contrast, low macro-contrast, diffuse cotton sheen.

5. **The seam-highlighter wash.** Pale halos following every seam, the neckband edge, the shoulder, the cuff and the hem, over broad tonal cloudiness. Described in the founder's own words. This is the thing that makes the garment look like a garment rather than a flat fill, and it maps directly onto a stroke-along-path lighten operation.

6. **The `0042e5` / `000000` / `f0f5ff` triad.** Three values, no greys, no tints, no gradients. An electric blue against pure black against a cold near-white. Brutally simple and extremely hard to make look bad. Note the rule: colour is celebrated *through* photography and type, not as decoration.

7. **The two-typeface tension.** Alexandria Regular (clean, geometric, freely available) carrying the argument, with The Old Falcons dropped in for exactly one payoff word per sentence. The whole voice of the brand lives in that switch.

8. **The embroidery spec as a graphic.** "Satin stitch filled-in lettering, zigzag stitching around letter outlines" at **2.5 × 1.58 in** in Bellwether Blue `#24315A` — and the artwork sheet showing the wordmark wrapped in its scalloped digitisation path. That bumpy outline is a beautiful device on its own.

9. **The tech-pack layout system.** Black flats with grey drape strokes on white, red arrow-ended callouts and measure lines, pipe-separated style strings, coloured circular trim tokens, rigid header/footer. Instantly legible as "real manufacturing", and nobody is using it as a compositional language.

10. **The scribble and scrawl layer.** Blue and black marker doodles — strokes, crosses, triangles, tick marks — laid under and over clean type; phrases struck through; "fashion" written over and over across a headline. Cheap to generate procedurally, high visual payoff.

11. **The real label objects.** The white ribbed woven label on black washed denim (macro, every weft cord visible); the 3 × 2.25 in white patch with black `digs` + "Designed in USA / Made in China" + size; the 1 × 2 in satin loop label caught in the side seam with its wall of care text and an empty `RN#` / `BATCH #:`. Small, dense, typographically real.

12. **The dress-form register.** Cream canvas size-40 form, aluminium neck cap, chrome stand, plain warm-white wall, one-sided window light. Unstyled and specific, and a much fresher backdrop than the stock street photography.

## 8. Contradictions and cautions

**The naming: DIGS vs "dope is good".**
Both are real and they are the same brand at two altitudes.
- **`digs`** (always lowercase) is the **wordmark and the product-facing name**. It's what's embroidered on the back yoke, printed on the neck label, printed on the hanger, and set in the tech-pack header. The style guide is titled `Digs_styleguide_boast.pdf`, calls the company "Digs" throughout, and gives the URL `www.digs.com`.
- **"Dope is good"** is the **phrase the name comes from and the slogan it's expressed as.** It appears as a poster headline on the style-guide cover, the repository itself is named `dopeisgood`, and the pant moodboard contains store-render mockups where the shopfront and the garments are branded **"DOPE IS GOOD"** in full.
- The brand strategy document supplies the *why*: the founder's stated mission is to *"redefine 'dope' as a symbol of positive change, empowerment, and quality of life."*
- **Unresolved:** nothing in the archive states the mechanical relationship — whether "digs" is an acronym of D-ope I-s G-ood-S, a contraction, or simply the English word "digs" chosen for the echo. The `DD` brandmark is described in the guide as abbreviating the logo, which doesn't map cleanly onto either name. Treat **`digs` as the mark, "dope is good" as the ethos line**, and don't over-claim the derivation.

**The style guide is a template, and it says so.** Final page, verbatim:
> *"PLEASE NOTE: PHOTOGRAPHY IN THIS DOC IS PLACEHOLDER ONLY UNTIL DIGS HAVE PRODUCED THEIR OWN ASSETS, AND MUST NOT BE USED EXTERNALLY OR COMMERCIALLY."*
Every model, every street shot, every lifestyle frame in the brand book is **stock placeholder**. The photographic art direction is aspirational, not proprietary. This is the single most important caution in the report.

**The guide's body copy is boilerplate and partly misassigned.** The "Logo Usage" page (p14) carries body text about *brandmark* clear space. Pages 9, 10, 12 and 13 repeat clear-space paragraphs under "Color Options" headings. The clear-space rule says to use the width of the **'S'** when the logo has no capital S — it means the lowercase s. Read the *diagrams*, not the prose.

**The script font is a demo.** The embedded font is `TheOldFalconsDemo` — a trial/demo cut. If you're reproducing the script, either license it properly or draw the letterforms.

**Two typos are baked into the artwork.** "Designed for those who push **boudaries**." and "Streetwear that echoes your **jorney**". Both are outlined vector, so they're in the mark, not a text-layer slip. Decide deliberately whether to reproduce or fix them.

**The brand palette and the product palette are different worlds.** The identity is electric blue `#0042e5` + black + ice. The actual garment is warm sage/khaki, off-white `#F8F0E8` and dusty sand blue `#6176A3`, with **navy** `#24315A` embroidery — not the brand blue. Nothing in the archive reconciles them. The one point of contact is that both are called blue.

**The brand-strategy document is visually unrelated to the brand.** `Brand strategy WIP - Antaeus Coe.pdf` is a mobile-page capture on a **bright yellow `#FEE600`** ground with warm cream `#E8E1D5` text pages. Neither colour appears anywhere in the identity. Treat it as a content source, not a visual one.

**The fibre and origin story reversed itself mid-development.** The spec moved 100% cotton (Preliminary) → **100% polyester** IRM Deformed Military Waffle (9.23.24 through 11.12.24) → back to **100% cotton** P9842 with a separate rib and a garment wash (6-15-25). Country of origin moved from *"Made in the USA of domestic and imported materials"* to *"Designed in USA / Made in China"*. **Legacy artwork in the 6-15-25 pack still shows the old "100% POLYESTER / Made in the USA" care label on the Interior View page while the Labels page shows the corrected one.** The current pack contradicts itself.
