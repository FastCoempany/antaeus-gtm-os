Design exploration · round 2 · not wired — three MORE summons, three different philosophies

Round one changed the gesture. Round two changes the philosophy.

The Ground / Birdseye / Compass were three gestures for the same idea: summon a map. These three ask a harder question — what if you never navigate by room name at all? One navigates by object (take the deal somewhere), one by physics (the map isn't summoned, it's revealed), one by language (say what you need; the system routes you). All live — try them.

Carry the deal. The deepest cut of all: you never go to a room — you take an object somewhere. Grab a deal (an account, a signal) and lift it off the page: the room falls away and the places that can act on that object appear as landing zones — run it to signature, pre-mortem it, run a pilot, get help on it. Drop it, and you land in that room with the deal already in hand. This is canon's first architectural truth ("object-first, not tab-first") turned into the navigation itself — the handoff strip made physical.

G4 — navigation disappears into the work: the question is never "which room" but "what does this deal need next." The zones are the rooms that ACCEPT the object (the compounding map §6, live). The full map stays one drop away ("somewhere else…"). Risk: only covers object-carrying jumps — needs a companion summon (the Ground) for "just take me to Settings."

Slide the room. No overlay, no panel, no summon at all — the map is not a UI that appears; it's the surface everything sits on. Grab the room anywhere on its open field and physically shove it — any direction, it follows your hand like a sheet of paper on a desk — and the motion map is simply there underneath, where it has been the whole time. Shove far enough and the room slides away; flick it back or tap "bring the room back." Nothing is ever "opened." You just look under the work.

G5 — the most tactile: direct manipulation of the room itself, desk physics, zero chrome. Philosophically the inverse of a palette: the map is never summoned because it never left. Pairs beautifully with the Ground story (the rooms literally SIT ON the motion). Risk: the grab must only arm on the open field — never fight text selection, row drags, or scroll.

Say what you need. No map, no zones, no metaphor — language. Just start typing, anywhere, in plain words: not a room name, but what you're trying to do — "get ramp signed" · "who's gone quiet" · "prep the call." The same command engine that ranks your board answers with routed moves, not room names — each one lands you in the right room with the right object already in hand. The most command-first navigation possible: you state intent, the system does the routing. Room names still work — they're just the shallow end.
