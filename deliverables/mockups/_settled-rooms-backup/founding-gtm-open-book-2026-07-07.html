§1 · Who we win
Who hits, who misses, and why
Ready to hand off

We win RevOps leaders at Series B–C companies who just hired a new VP of Sales. Every time. The new VP wants a number they can trust in 90 days, and RevOps is holding the bag — so they move fast and they have budget. That's the shape of every deal we've closed.

The misses are the same mistake in reverse: a curious IC with no budget, at a company where nothing just changed. Good conversations, no close.

Won · RevOps · new sales leadership · quarter-end — 5 of 6 won
Miss · IC / analyst · no trigger — 4 of 5 lost
One thing to notice
Your written ICP is broader than the deals you actually win.
Saved target is "B2B SaaS, 50–500." Every win was RevOps right after a sales-leadership change. Narrow it to what closes.
§2 · Outreach that worked
The outreach that actually landed a meeting
Ready to hand off

The opener that works names the new VP and references the leadership change out loud, then a call two days later off the email. Email alone gets ignored; the call alone doesn't work cold. It's the pair that lands — 34% reply vs 6% for generic notes.

Top play · email → call, leadership-change opener, RevOps — 34% reply, 9 meetings
One thing to notice
You've never tried LinkedIn on the people you actually win.
Your 3 hottest untouched accounts have RevOps leaders active on LinkedIn — and zero touches there.

§4 · Won & where we leak
Where deals are won, and where they leak
Ready to hand off

Deals are won at the first meeting — if the person in the room owns the number, we close 60% of the time. We leak at security review: 4 of 6 losses stalled there and never restarted. We don't lose the argument — we go quiet, and the deal cools.

Biggest leak · security review — 4 of 6 losses stalled, none advised
One thing to notice
Every deal that leaked at security had no advisor on it.
You have investors who've cleared enterprise security reviews. None were pulled in on the four deals that died there.

§6 · Why we win
Why we win
Ready to hand off

When we win, one thing is always true: the champion is the person who owns the number. They feel the pressure personally, so they pull the deal through their own procurement instead of waiting on us.

If you remember one thing walking in: find the person who gets asked about the number, and make them your champion. Everything else follows.

Shared trait of every win · champion owns the number + live trigger + dated next step from meeting one
