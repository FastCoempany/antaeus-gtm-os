Round three: who's doing the navigating?

Round 1 changed the gesture. Round 2 changed the philosophy. Round 3 changes who initiates — one where you surrender the choice entirely, one where the house calls you, and one where the door isn't a room at all — it's the object's own life. All live.

The Current. The most radical reduction possible: navigation collapses to one word. You never choose a room. When you're done where you are, you say next — one quiet word at the foot of every room (or tap N) — and the current carries you to wherever the pressure is highest, telling you why as you travel. The command engine already ranks the whole workspace; this makes that ranking the ONLY navigation. Total surrender to command-first: the system is harder to fool than you are, so let it drive.

G7 — peak command-first: the ranked board becomes the ONLY door. Beautiful discipline: you stop deciding "where do I go" forever (the exact cognitive loop canon calls the hallway). It pairs with ANY map summon as the escape hatch. Risk: surrender needs trust — the carry must always say why, and stepping off must always be one key.

Thin Walls. Navigation you never initiate. The other rooms are always there, on the other side of the walls — and when something in one of them needs you, its pressure bleeds through: a red glow on the right wall is a deal dying in Future Autopsy; amber above is your pace slipping in Quota Workback; blue on the left is the Briefing holding three reads you haven't seen. Rest on a glow and the wall whispers what's behind it; press through and you're there. The house is quiet when nothing needs you — silence is the healthy state.

G8 — the inverse of every other summon: the SYSTEM initiates, ambiently, with light instead of notifications. Deeply canon-native — escalation only when real (rule 7), pressure legible before words, and the calm state is genuinely calm (a quiet house). Could layer UNDER any chosen summon. Risk: glows must be rationed ruthlessly or they become the noise they replaced.

Follow the Object. The door isn't a room — it's the object's own life. Every account or deal name, anywhere it appears, is a quiet door: click Ramp and you see Ramp's whole thread through the house — its heat in Signal Console, its touches in Outbound, the discovery call it came from, the deal at terms, the favor you could call in. One thread, every room that holds a piece of it, jump to any stop. Canon says objects carry context between rooms — this makes the object itself the hallway-killer: you don't browse rooms, you follow the thing you care about.

G9 — object-first taken to its logical end: rooms become invisible plumbing and the object's thread is the navigation. The inverse of G4 (carry = push the object somewhere; follow = trace where it already lives). Kills "where was that thing" forever. Risk: needs the cross-room reads to be live (they are — the rooms already publish them); and room-level jumps still need a fallback summon.
