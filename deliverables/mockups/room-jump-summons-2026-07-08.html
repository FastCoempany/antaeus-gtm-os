Design exploration · not wired · the SUMMON itself, reimagined — no Ctrl+K, no chord, no rail

How you call the map matters as much as the map.

Ctrl+K is the generic developer idiom — every SaaS has it. Antaeus can do better, and the answer is sitting in the brand: Antaeus was unbeatable while he touched the ground — and the brand mark's signature element is literally the ground line. Three summons, all mouse-first, all summoned-then-gone, none a rail. Each demo is live — try the gesture.

The Ground. Every room quietly stands on a ground line — the same stroke the brand mark stands on, almost invisible, at the very foot of the screen. When you need your bearings, touch the ground: click the line (or press G) and the motion map rises from beneath the room — because the map IS the ground the rooms are built on. Click a room or press Esc and it sinks back. The myth is the mechanism: touching the ground is how Antaeus gets his strength back.

G1 — the strongest brand story: the mark's signature stroke becomes the app's one navigation gesture. Nearly invisible until wanted (no rail, no chrome). Rises from BELOW — nothing else in the app does that, so the motion itself means "ground." Keyboard: G or Esc. Risk: the line must stay whisper-quiet so it never reads as chrome.

Birdseye. No overlay at all — an altitude change. Click the Antaeus mark (top-left of every room) or press Esc, and the room you're in lifts away beneath you: it shrinks back into its place on the motion map and the whole territory fades in around it, you-are-here lit. Click any room to descend into it. This makes the app one continuous place instead of pages — and it makes the existing "birdseye" idea literal: the float you already have becomes real altitude. Navigation and the "what should I look at next" read become the same view.

G2 — the most conceptually radical: rooms stop being pages and become places on one map; navigating = changing altitude. Unifies the birdseye float + the map into ONE organ (the float's "what to look at next" is simply what glows when you rise). Risk: the lift animation must be fast + optional (reduced-motion falls back to a crossfade); Esc-to-rise needs care inside inputs.

The Compass. Nothing on screen at all. Press and hold anywhere on the open field (a beat, ~a quarter second) and a compass blooms at your cursor — the six stages of the motion around a hub. Slide to a stage and its rooms fan out; click to jump; release on empty space and it's gone. The menu comes to where your hand already is — zero travel, zero chrome, and with muscle memory it becomes the fastest jump possible.
