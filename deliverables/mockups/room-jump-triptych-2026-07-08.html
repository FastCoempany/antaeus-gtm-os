Three directions for what it could become (shortcut shown as Ctrl+K for Windows/Linux; it renders ⌘K on Mac), in the new language. Click to compare.

The motion map. Instead of an alphabetical list, the 22 rooms are laid out as your actual GTM motion — left to right, entry → strategy → find & work → advance → diagnose → hand off. You see where you are, where the system thinks you should go next, and the whole shape at once. Summoned with Ctrl+K as a bright overlay; type to filter, or just point at the stage you want.

The command line, reborn. The Ctrl+K palette you have — but in the new language: a serif prompt, de-carded rows, each room named in spoon-fed words with its family tag, skills woven in with a round blue mark. The empty state is smart — your recent rooms + the one room the system thinks you need next, before you type a letter. Keyboard-first and fast.

B — the smallest leap from today; keeps the muscle memory (Ctrl+K, type, Enter) but dresses it in the redesign + adds a smart default so most jumps need zero typing. Risk: still a "list", just a nicer one.

The persistent switcher. Not summoned at all — a slim, collapsible rail that's always there on the left edge. The 8 families as a compact spine, your current room lit, the system's suggested next room pinned at the top. One click to jump; you never lose track of where you are. It folds today's three separate organs — the Ctrl+K palette, the birdseye "what to look at next" float, and the back-trail — into one nav organ.
