The eye lands left and reads the room's pressure before reading a word. Hover a tick and its row lights; click and you're there. Annotation, never navigation.

LM-1 — the closest to real marginalia: it comments on exactly what's on screen, scales to any room with ranked rows, and costs ~14px. The risk is tick-blindness on healthy rooms — a column of quiet forest reads as texture. Strongest on the Diagnosis Table rooms where state varies.

LM-2 · The pressure spine. A 4px strip on the far left that is the whole page mapped to scroll — every row's state painted at its scroll position, with a small window showing where you are. Glance left: how much red is below the fold? Click anywhere on the spine to jump. A minimap with no map. Scroll the room and watch the window ride.

LM-2 — answers the one question a tall room can't: "is there pressure I can't see right now?" It's honest escalation (canon rule 7) — red only exists where red is real. Quietest win: it doubles as a scrollbar the eye actually reads. Risk: on short rooms it's a solution without a problem.

LM-3 · The provenance whisper. The continuity params made visible: faint rotated text running up the left edge — where you came from, and what rides along. The first thing the eye asks when it jumps left is "where am I, how did I get here." This answers without one pixel of chrome. Hover warms it; click takes you back.

LM-3 — the philosophical twin of the back-pill, but it lives where the eye starts instead of where the chrome is. Reading rotated text is a real cost (nobody loves tilting their head), so it works as ambient reassurance you *feel* rather than copy you read — and the params already exist, so it's nearly free.

LM-4 · Dog-ears. Two or three tiny folded corners at the left edge — the objects you touched last, like fingers held in a book. Hover names them; click opens the Follow peek (in the real build) — here it lights a toast. "Pick up where your thumb was." Recency, not hierarchy.
