# The Antaeus Teaser — four directions

**Date:** 2026-07-23
**Status:** CONCEPT — nothing built. Four distinct directions for founder pick.
**What this is:** the cinematic-teaser treatment of Antaeus — the game-trailer
grammar, not the product-demo grammar. No full app pages, no cursor, no browser
anywhere. The viewer is made to *imagine* the app; the live Luca demo stays the
payoff they ask for afterward.

---

## Shared foundations (all four directions obey these)

**The teaser laws:**
1. **Shards, never screens.** Interface fragments staged as objects — oversized,
   isolated, lit, with depth. Nothing that reads as "a web page." Nothing that
   could be screenshot-compared to the real app.
2. **The product's voice is the script.** Every line of text on screen is a
   sentence the app itself would say — plain, severe, spoon-fed (§11/§13).
   No marketing vocabulary anywhere. The severity is the drama.
3. **One thing at a time.** A teaser frame holds one idea. The cut, not the
   layout, carries density.
4. **Real names.** Boeing, Starbucks, OpenAI do narrative work no fictional
   name can. The roster is part of the cinematography.
5. **The brand system is the art direction.** The field (#f6f8fc), navy ink,
   the one orange, forest for what endures, DM Serif Display for consequence,
   mono for the machine's whispers, the Grounded-A, the ground line. The teaser
   should be recognizable as Antaeus from any single frame.
6. **End on the itch.** Title, one line, `antaeus.app`. Never a feature recap.

**Runtime target:** 60–90 seconds. **Aspect:** 16:9, cut-down-able to 1:1/9:16
shards for social. **Sound:** designed for mute-first (text carries everything);
music/VO are a layer on top, not a dependency.

## D1 · THE SHARDS
*Interface cinematography. A gallery of a machine's parts.*

**Logline.** A slow camera drifts through a dark space where fragments of an
unseen instrument hang like exhibits — each one alive, each one implying the
whole. You leave knowing the machine exists, without ever seeing it.

**The organizing idea.** Space instead of time. No story, no protagonist, no
chronology — five exhibits in one continuous dolly move, connected by a thin
line of light the camera follows (the eventual ground line, though it's never
named). The confidence of the piece IS the pitch: a team that can stage its
product like this has taste, and taste is what a teaser sells.

**The five exhibits (in camera order):**
1. **The Pick.** Out of a field of dim, drifting account names (dozens — the
   noise of a real book), one ignites: `BOEING`. A serif line assembles beside
   it: *"The one move that matters. Picked. With the reason attached."* Below,
   small mono: `heat 62 · fresh signals · next step due in 3 days`.
2. **The Wire.** A vertical thread of plain sentences, stamped `YOU` / `THE
   MACHINE` / `THE BUYERS`, sliding past in the dark. One arrives glowing
   orange: *"Starbucks replied after 20 quiet days."* A giant number ticks
   13 → 14.
3. **The Thread.** The word `BOEING` again — and from it, a line draws itself
   through five floating waypoints: *the signal → the message → the call →
   the deal → the favor.* Serif: *"One account. Its whole life. One thread."*
4. **The Face-off.** Two massive columns of type slide in from opposite edges:
   THEIR SIDE — *"150 security questions before anything moves."* YOUR LINE —
   *"Your papers already answer 118."* A thin bar fills between them, 118
   segments lighting in forest green.
5. **The Book.** The fragments from every prior exhibit — the pick, the wire
   lines, the thread, the bar — drift together and settle into the pages of an
   open book. Serif: *"Everything compounds into what your first hire opens on
   day one."*

**Close.** The thin light line the camera has been following runs level across
black. The Grounded-A settles onto it. `ANTAEUS`. One line: *"The revenue
system a first hire can run."* `antaeus.app`.

**Palette note.** This is the one direction allowed to live mostly dark — a
staged theater space, not a product surface (the product stays bright; the
*stage* is dark, like a museum at night). Brand accents unchanged.

**Distinct because:** no time, no myth, no human. Pure object-confidence.
**Best for:** audiences who reward craft — design-literate VCs, product Twitter.
**Risk:** cold. It impresses more than it moves. Nobody tears up at a gallery.

## D2 · THE MYTH
*Strength from the ground. The brand's origin story as the film.*

**Logline.** A mark hangs in the air, weak and hollow. It touches the earth and
strength returns — and everything the earth knows rises with it. The Antaeus
myth told straight, with the product appearing only as the myth's consequence.

**The organizing idea.** Allegory-first. For the first half, the viewer doesn't
know they're watching a software teaser — they're watching a fable about
groundedness. The product elements never get introduced; they *grow*. This is
the only direction where the film's logic is the brand's logic: lifted = the
motion living in one head; grounded = the motion living in a system.

**The four movements:**
1. **LIFTED.** Near-white emptiness. The Grounded-A floats high, tilted,
   hollow — stroke thinned, barely there. Beneath it, far down, a faint
   horizon line. Serif lines fade in and out around it, each one a weight:
   *"Every deal, in one head." "Every reason, remembered by one person."
   "Every quarter, started from zero."* The mark drifts lower under each line.
2. **TOUCH.** The mark's feet meet the line. One frame of stillness. Then the
   contact point pulses — and the line wakes: it runs out in both directions,
   gains weight, becomes THE ground line. Mono whispers ripple along it:
   `watching · counting · remembering`.
3. **RISE.** From the line, things grow upward like the Ground summon rising —
   but as silhouettes and fragments, never panels: a stem that becomes a
   ticking count; a thread that blooms into five waypoints around the word
   `BOEING`; a bar that fills 118 of 150; a red serif line — *"3 deals will
   slip this week if you do nothing."* — that resolves into an orange one:
   *"Here's the move."* The mark stands at center through all of it, solid
   now, full-stroke, planted.
4. **STAND.** Everything grown recedes into the line, leaving the mark
   standing on it — and beside it, a second, smaller mark rises out of the
   ground and lands on the line too. Serif: *"Built so the next person can
   stand on it."* (The first hire, in the myth's own language.)

**Close.** `ANTAEUS` · *"Strength, from the ground."* · `antaeus.app`.

**Distinct because:** the product is never shown, only *implied by growth* —
the purest teaser of the four, and the only one with an emotional arc.
**Best for:** brand-building, the fundraise narrative, the landing page hero.
**Risk:** abstraction. A literal-minded viewer may finish moved but unsure
what the software does. (The counter: that's what the meeting is for.)

## D3 · THE DAY
*One working day, read entirely off the instruments. The operator is never seen.*

**Logline.** 6:04 AM to 11:47 PM. You never see a person and never see a
screen — you see the traces a working day leaves on a living system: counts
ticking, replies landing, silence surfacing, a book quietly writing itself.
By midnight you know someone extraordinary works here, and something
extraordinary works *for* them.

**The organizing idea.** Chronology as structure, absence as the device. A
small mono timestamp in the corner is the film's metronome; every beat is
"what the system saw at this hour." It's the Live Edge doctrine — accumulator,
honest, three voices — expanded to feature length. The warmest of the four:
this is the direction that gets scored with real music and feels like a film
about a person, even though no person appears.

**The clock beats:**
- `6:04 AM` — a dot starts breathing. Mono: `the workspace is awake`.
- `7:31 AM` — serif, alone: *"Here's the one move that matters today."*
  Then, small: `Outbound to Boeing — the reason attached.`
- `9:12 AM` — the count ticks 4 → 5 → 6. *"Captured. Counted. Nobody logged
  anything."*
- `10:48 AM` — orange ignition: *"Starbucks replied — after 20 quiet days."*
- `1:15 PM` — the machine's voice, blue-stamped: *"Calendar — Thursday with
  United is confirmed."*
- `3:40 PM` — the honest beat, red: *"Nordstrom has no dated next step.
  21 days quiet."* (The system that never flatters.)
- `6:22 PM` — the face-off fragment: 118 of 150, filling.
- `11:47 PM` — everything the day produced — the ticks, the reply, the
  confirms, the warning — drifts down like sediment into an open book.
  *"And every day settles into the thing that matters: what your first hire
  opens on day one."*

**Close.** The dot still breathing in the dark. `ANTAEUS` · *"It runs while
you work."* · `antaeus.app`.

**Distinct because:** time-structured, human-warm, music-forward; the only
direction with suspense (what lands next?) rather than spectacle.
**Best for:** emotional resonance with operators; the version a founder-buyer
forwards to another founder.
**Risk:** quietest of the four. Needs impeccable rhythm or it reads slow.

## D4 · THE GROUNDED CUT  ← the recommended synthesis
*The Myth as the frame. The Shards as the language. The Day's clock as the pulse.*

**Logline.** A weakened mark falls to earth; the moment it touches, a working
day ignites along the ground line — told in shards, timed by a clock — and by
nightfall what grew from the ground has become something a second pair of feet
can stand on.

**Why the hybrid beats its parents.** The Myth alone risks abstraction; the
Shards alone risk coldness; the Day alone risks slowness. Frame + language +
pulse solves all three: the myth gives the film a beginning and an end that
*mean* something, the shards give the middle its spectacle and product truth,
and the clock gives the whole thing a heartbeat that keeps it moving. It is
also the only version where all three of the brand's deepest assets — the
name's story, the design system, the Live Edge doctrine — appear on screen
as one continuous thought.

**The beat sheet (75 seconds):**

**ACT I — LIFTED (0:00–0:12).** Bright emptiness. The hollow mark floats high,
tilted. Two serif truths, one at a time: *"Your whole revenue motion lives in
one head."* … *"Yours."* The mark sinks with each. A faint line waits below.
No clock yet — time hasn't started.

**ACT II — TOUCH (0:12–0:18).** Feet meet line. Stillness. The pulse of
contact. The line wakes and runs out to both edges. In the corner, small mono
ignites for the first time: `6:04 AM`. The film now has a heartbeat.

**ACT III — THE DAY RISES (0:18–0:58).** The middle is the Shards sequence —
but every shard *grows out of the ground line*, and every cut advances the
clock. The exhibits become hours:
- `7:31 AM` — **the Pick** rises: dim account names drift up like heat off
  the line; `BOEING` ignites; *"The one move that matters. Picked. With the
  reason attached."*
- `9:12 AM` — **the Wire** climbs: the tagged sentences stack upward, the
  count ticks 13 → 14 at the top of the stem. *"Captured. Counted. Nobody
  logged anything."*
- `10:48 AM` — **the ignition**: orange floods one line — *"Starbucks
  replied after 20 quiet days."* The biggest single frame of the film.
- `2:20 PM` — **the Thread**: `BOEING` again, the line drawing through its
  five waypoints. *"One account. Its whole life. One thread."*
- `4:45 PM` — **the honest beat**, red, no decoration: *"3 deals will slip
  this week if you do nothing."* Beat. Orange answer: *"Here's the move."*
- `6:22 PM` — **the Face-off**: their 150 questions vs your 118 answers,
  the forest bar filling between them.
- `11:47 PM` — **the settling**: everything the day grew leans, loosens, and
  sediments down the stems into an open book resting ON the ground line.
  *"Everything compounds into what your first hire opens on day one."*

**ACT IV — STAND (0:58–1:15).** The book closes. The line is quiet. The mark
stands planted at center — full-stroke, solid. Then the myth's last turn: a
second, smaller mark rises from beneath the line and sets its feet beside the
first. Serif: *"Built so the next person can stand on it."* Cut to black.
`ANTAEUS` · *"Strength, from the ground."* · `antaeus.app`. The mono clock
gives its final read, tiny, under the URL: `6:04 AM tomorrow`.

**The three signatures that make it ours alone:**
1. Nothing appears except by growing from the ground line (the myth enforced
   as a *layout rule* — no element may enter frame from above or the sides).
2. The clock never lies about pace — it jumps the way a real day jumps,
   clustering in the morning, going quiet mid-afternoon (the accumulator
   doctrine, felt as rhythm).
3. The only pure-orange frame in the film is the reply landing. Rule 7 —
   escalation — as cinematography.

**Distinct because:** it's the only version with a beginning that means
something, a middle that shows something, and an end that promises something.
**Risk:** ambition — it's the hardest cut to pace. The mitigation is that all
of its parts are testable alone (each act is a shard-scene the proven camera
rig can shoot and re-shoot independently).

## How they compare

| | D1 Shards | D2 Myth | D3 Day | D4 Grounded Cut |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Structure | space (a gallery) | allegory (a fable) | time (a clock) | fable framing a clocked gallery |
| Emotional register | awe | meaning | warmth | all three, sequenced |
| Product visibility | high (fragments) | lowest | medium | high, inside the story |
| Hardest craft problem | avoiding coldness | avoiding vagueness | avoiding slowness | pacing three grammars |
| Social cut-downs | excellent (each exhibit) | weak (needs the arc) | good (each hour) | excellent (each hour-shard) |
| One-frame test* | passes | passes | passes | passes |

*Any paused frame should be recognizably Antaeus and §13-legible cold.

## Production note (for later, not now)

All four shoot on the pipeline already proven this week: a purpose-built
animated stage (the brand's own type, color, and motion, authored as its own
canvas — zero app screenshots) driven scene-by-scene and filmed off the
virtual display at 30fps, cut in ffmpeg. Music/VO land in any editor
afterward. Each direction's scenes are independently re-shootable, so
iteration stays cheap after the pick.
