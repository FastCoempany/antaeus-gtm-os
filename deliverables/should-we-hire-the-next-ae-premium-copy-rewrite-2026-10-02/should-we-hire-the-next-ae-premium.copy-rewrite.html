What a wrong hire costs

A wrong AE hire costs roughly $200–300k. For the leader who asked for it, it can cost them their job.

A hiring plan usually counts only the salary. Add the ramp time, the recruiting and the pipeline that never closes, and the cost of a hire your sales process couldn’t support adds up fast.

  1. Salary and commission while they ramp

    Months of base pay, and often a guaranteed commission, before the first deal of their own closes.

  2. Recruiting

    Agency fees or recruiter time, plus the interview hours pulled out of your team’s week.

  3. Pipeline handed over that doesn’t close

    Accounts given to a new seller when there wasn’t enough pipeline to keep them busy. Many are hard to restart once the seller leaves.

  4. Your team’s time

    Onboarding, call reviews and the manager hours spent trying to make the hire work.

What the brief is really checking

Does your sales process still work without the person who built it?

Antaeus is named for the giant of Greek myth who was strong while his feet touched the earth and weak when he was lifted off it. A sales process works the same way: it holds up when it rests on things anyone can see and use, and fails when it depends on one person.

The brief tells you which one you have before you hire another AE onto it.

How it works

The analysis does the math. A person helps you understand it.

Every brief applies the same four tests to your numbers. Each comes back with a plain answer, and the brief names the one that’s holding the hire back.

  1. Test 1

    Economic need

    Does your revenue plan actually need more selling capacity than the team you already have?

  2. Test 2

    Pipeline supply

    Is there enough qualified pipeline for one more seller without taking deals your current sellers need?

  3. Test 3

    Repeatable without the founder

    Can someone other than the founder win deals the same way, often enough to plan on?

  4. Test 4

    Timing and management

    Can the hire ramp in time to help this year’s revenue target, with a manager who has the hours to coach them?

The analysis

Included in every brief
  1. Works backward from your target to the wins, qualified opportunities and pipeline the new AE would need.
  2. Flags numbers that contradict each other, like a win rate that can’t produce the target, or a sales cycle longer than the time you have.
  3. Shows its work. Every number in the brief can be traced back to something you entered.

What you get

The brief gives you one clear answer and the reasoning behind it.

The answer is one of four

  • Supported

    Your numbers support another AE now.

  • Supported with conditions

    The numbers work, but something has to change first, and the brief names what.

  • Not yet supported

    Your sales process can’t support another AE yet. The brief says what would change that.

  • Not enough information yet

    Too many numbers are unknown to give an answer. The brief lists exactly what to go find out.

What’s in the brief

  • The decision

    One of the four, stated in a sentence you can repeat to your CEO or your board.

  • The one thing limiting it

    Named plainly, like “there isn’t enough qualified pipeline yet for another seller.”

  • The math, worked backward from your target

    From your revenue target down to the deals to win, qualified opportunities and pipeline the new AE needs.

  • What has to be true before you hire

    Conditions you can check off, not general advice.

  • What’s assumed and what’s missing

    Every “I don’t know” is listed as missing. None is filled in with an average.

  • What would change the answer

    The number that would change the answer fastest if it were different, so you know where to look first.

It can say no

Not yet supported

Some briefs come back “not yet.” That answer is still worth paying for.

A “not yet” names the exact thing that has to change first: more qualified pipeline, a win rate someone other than the founder can repeat, or a manager with time to coach. You walk away knowing what to fix, and once you’ve fixed it, you’ll have the numbers to back the request the next time you ask for the role.

The brief won’t stretch the numbers to give you the yes you were hoping for. Hearing “not yet” before the offer goes out costs a lot less than hearing it from the board a year later.

Questions

Questions before you buy

Who reads my numbers?

On the reviewed brief and the before-the-board brief, I do. I’m the founder, and I read your numbers myself before we talk. The self-serve brief is the analysis on its own: the math, the numbers it flags as not agreeing, and the decision, with no person reading it.

What if the answer is no?

Then the brief says “not yet” and names what has to become true first. That’s a real answer, and it’s often the more useful one. The brief won’t stretch your numbers to reach a yes.

How is this different from a headcount calculator?

A calculator takes your numbers and multiplies them. The analysis also checks whether they agree with each other, for example whether your win rate and pipeline can actually produce the target, and it can tell you not to hire. The reviewed brief adds a person who has watched this decision play out at five companies.

What do I need to have ready?

Your revenue target, your current sellers and their quota, your typical deal size, your win rate, your sales cycle, and how much new pipeline you create each month.

If you don’t know one, say “I don’t know.” That’s treated as information and listed in the brief. It’s never filled in with an industry average.

What happens to my numbers?

They’re used only for your brief and, on the reviewed and before-the-board briefs, for our call. The full data policy, covering how your answers are stored, who can see them and how they’re deleted, will be published before checkout opens.

Is this a recruiting service?

No. Antaeus doesn’t find, screen or place candidates. The brief answers whether your company should add the role, not who should fill it.
