# Antaeus Copy Standard

**Version:** 1.0  
**Purpose:** Make Antaeus understandable on first read without flattening its voice.  
**Primary target:** semantic-compression saturation <= 10/100 across marketing, product demonstrations, navigation, comparisons, FAQ, and interface microcopy.

---

## 1. Governing principle

Antaeus copy must optimize for **maximum useful information per inference required**.

A reader should not have to translate Antaeus language into ordinary English before understanding what the product does.

Every consequential statement should make four things explicit whenever they matter:

1. **What happened or exists.**
2. **What Antaeus did with it.**
3. **Why it matters.**
4. **What the user can do next.**

Do not omit one of these merely to make the sentence feel sharper.

---

## 2. Default language rule

Use the most literal noun and verb that accurately describes the product behavior.

Prefer:

- account
- deal
- buyer
- stakeholder
- activity
- recommendation
- order (as in "in order of risk" — describing what the product does; see §26)
- evidence
- blocker
- risk
- workflow
- product area
- sales process
- sales activity
- target customer
- revenue target
- benchmark
- follow-up question
- buyer response
- workspace data

Do not substitute a metaphor when the literal noun is available.

---

## 3. Terms that require scrutiny

These words are not globally banned. They are banned **when used as a proxy for a more precise product concept**.

| Scrutinize | Usually replace with |
|---|---|
| motion | sales process, sales workflow, sales activity, GTM process |
| move | action, recommendation, task, next step |
| moved | changed, replied, advanced, opened roles, hired, became inactive; name the actual event |
| room | product area, workflow, tool, module; or the actual named feature |
| shape | structure, workflow, call structure, configuration |
| line | message, question, response, talking point, recommended follow-up |
| hot / warm / cold | high priority, recent trigger, recent engagement, no recent activity, declining engagement |
| slip | miss a target date, lose momentum, fall below plan; specify which |
| die | be lost, become inactive, fail to close; specify which |
| run | execute, use, manage, operate; specify what is being run |
| carry | retain, transfer, attach, reuse |
| ground | use only as the proper product name **The Ground**, never as an unexplained generic concept |
| edge | use only as the proper product name **The Live Edge**, never as an unexplained generic concept |
| in your head | undocumented founder knowledge / founder-only context |
| awake | configured and actively processing data |
| owned the number | directly accountable for the relevant business metric or budget |
| had to go ask | required approval from another decision-maker |

When the precise meaning differs by context, resolve the context. Do not perform blind word substitution.

---

## 4. Product names are allowed; private grammar is not

Antaeus can have distinctive feature names. A proper name does not give the surrounding sentence permission to become metaphorical.

Allowed examples:

- **The Briefing** — followed by a literal description of the morning summary.
- **Quota Workback** — followed by a literal description of revenue-target planning and benchmark checking. (Founder-settled 2026-11-05: this is the area's name; "Pace" is not used as a name for it.)
- **The Ground** — followed immediately by “the workspace navigation map” or equivalent on first mention.
- **The Live Edge** — followed immediately by “the collapsible activity panel” or equivalent on first mention.
- **Discovery Studio** — followed by a literal description of live discovery-call guidance.
- **Future Autopsy** — may remain a feature name only if its surrounding copy says explicitly that it projects deal-loss risk.

Rule: **A branded name may be memorable. Its definition must be boringly clear.**

Do not create additional capitalized concepts unless they name an actual stable object in the product.

---

## 5. Ban the private-ontology cascade

Do not construct paragraphs in which several ordinary concepts are all replaced by Antaeus metaphors.

### Reject

> Open one room and read the state of the whole motion in a sentence, then the single thing worth doing before lunch.

### Prefer

> Open any product area to see the current state of your accounts and deals, then the highest-priority action for the day.

One branded term can coexist with literal language. Several stacked metaphors cannot.

---

## 6. Restore causal connective tissue

Antaeus frequently has real evidence. State the relationship between evidence and conclusion.

Do not rely on juxtaposition to imply causality.

### Reject

> Two plants announced. A few hundred hires. No recruiting bench. Running hot.

### Prefer

> Chomps announced two plants that will require several hundred hires. Because the company appears to have limited recruiting capacity, Antaeus marks the account as a high-priority outreach opportunity.

Use words such as:

- because
- based on
- which means
- therefore
- compared with
- while
- after
- before
- according to

when they make the reasoning explicit.

---

## 7. Evidence -> interpretation -> action

Recommendations should usually follow this sequence:

**Evidence**  
What changed or what the workspace contains.

**Interpretation**  
What Antaeus concludes from the evidence.

**Action**  
What the user should do next.

### Example

**Evidence:** Chomps announced two plants and several hundred planned hires. Your workspace shows no prior outreach.  
**Interpretation:** This is a recent hiring trigger, and outreach is currently your least-completed sales activity.  
**Action:** Contact Chomps first.

The copy can compress this sequence, but it may not remove the causal relationship.

---

## 8. Pronoun and placeholder test

Challenge every consequential use of:

- it
- this
- that
- one
- thing
- something
- someone
- what
- where
- they

Keep the word only when its referent is unmistakable within the same sentence or the immediately preceding one.

### Reject

> The single most valuable one, with its reason shown.

### Prefer

> The highest-priority action, with the evidence for why it comes first.

### Reject

> Sorted by what just moved.

### Prefer

> Ranked by recent account activity and company changes.

---

## 9. Personification rule

Do not describe software as if it has human perception or judgment when a literal system behavior is available.

Avoid, unless literally necessary:

- sees
- notices
- thinks
- knows
- wakes
- remembers
- flinches
- hands you
- reads your mind / reads you back

Prefer:

- reviews
- analyzes
- detects
- compares
- sorts / puts in order (never "ranks"; see §26)
- records
- surfaces
- displays
- drafts
- recommends

“Antaeus reads workspace data” is acceptable only when “read” refers plainly to machine analysis of data. “It reads your whole way of selling back to you” is not.

---

## 10. No insight voice

Do not write a sentence primarily to sound like a distilled truth.

The information should create the insight; cadence should not impersonate insight.

### Reject

> Where a deal says it is matters less than what it is actually stuck on.

### Prefer

> Antaeus evaluates deal risk using unresolved blockers and buyer activity, not pipeline stage alone.

### Reject

> The inbox is never the opening.

### Prefer

> Do not begin the sequence with a direct message; build context before asking for time.

Short sentences, contrast, and confidence are allowed. Pseudo-profundity is not.

---

## 11. Headline standard

A headline must be understandable without the paragraph above it.

A cold reader should be able to answer: **What is this section telling me?**

### Headline requirements

- Name the product capability, user outcome, or concrete question.
- Avoid pronouns whose antecedent sits elsewhere on the page.
- Avoid metaphor as the sole carrier of meaning.
- Avoid paired slogan fragments when a literal sentence would be clearer.
- Keep the strongest concrete noun in the headline.

### Reject

> You built it. They can run it.

### Prefer

> Turn the sales process you built into a system your first hire can use.

---

## 12. Navigation standard

Navigation labels name destinations, not themes.

Prefer:

- Product areas
- Sales handoff
- Activity monitoring
- Revenue plan
- Comparison
- Beta limitations

Avoid navigation labels that require prior knowledge of the brand vocabulary.

Feature names can appear inside the destination page after the destination is clear.

---

## 13. Product-demo copy standard

Product demonstrations are not advertising space. They should behave like real software.

Requirements:

- use literal status labels;
- name the event that changed;
- name the reason something comes first;
- name the blocker;
- state units and time frames;
- use verbs that describe actual system behavior;
- prefer compact data language over metaphor.

### Reject

> Act now — hot, and something just moved

### Prefer

> High priority · recent company change

### Reject

> champion went quiet

### Prefer

> no champion activity for 14 days

---

## 14. Interface microcopy standard

Microcopy should be shorter than marketing copy and more literal.

Use:

- noun + state
- verb + object
- number + unit
- event + time

Examples:

- `Highest priority · recent trigger`
- `No buyer activity · 14 days`
- `3 deals at risk`
- `Recommended follow-up`
- `Open deal`
- `Export workspace`

Do not use a metaphor simply because the label is small.

---

## 15. Comparison standard

Comparison copy must identify the concrete capability being compared.

Prefer:

- “Puts next actions in order and shows the evidence for why each one is where it is.”
- “Stores activity and pipeline data for reporting.”
- “Manual tracking configured by the user.”

Avoid:

- “why that one”
- “whatever you decided it was for”
- “only in your head”
- clever dismissals that replace the actual comparison dimension.

Comparisons may be opinionated in positioning, but the rows themselves must remain interpretable as product claims.

---

## 16. FAQ standard

The FAQ is the decoding layer. It must be the most literal writing on the page.

Requirements:

- answer the question in the first sentence;
- use ordinary product vocabulary;
- define proprietary terms instead of repeating them;
- distinguish product facts from positioning claims;
- do not use a metaphor to explain another metaphor;
- avoid slogans in the answer.

If a visitor reaches the FAQ because the main page was unclear, the FAQ cannot require them to learn more Antaeus vocabulary.

---

## 17. State-name standard

Readiness states must describe an observable condition.

Recommended labels:

1. **Founder-dependent**
2. **Partially documented**
3. **Usable with founder support**
4. **Hire-ready**
5. **Hire-ready and repeatable**

Avoid:

- You are the system
- Building
- Inheritable

unless those phrases appear as secondary flavor text rather than the primary state label.

---

## 18. Punctuation rule

Do not use punctuation to hide a logical relationship.

Middle dots and em dashes are fine for compact metadata, but they do not replace conjunctions or causal language in prose.

### Good

`High priority · new COO hired 2 days ago`

### Bad

`Two plants announced · no recruiting bench · running hot`

The second example asks punctuation to perform reasoning.

---

## 19. Sentence-fragment rule

Fragments are allowed only when they function as labels, controls, or compact UI states.

Marketing paragraphs and explanations should use complete propositions.

Allowed:

- `Highest priority`
- `No activity · 14 days`
- `Open deal`

Reject:

- `The single most valuable one, with its reason shown.`
- `The autopsy, early.`
- `One motion. Two people who run it.`

---

## 20. Specificity test

Whenever the copy says any of the following, ask whether the page already knows the specific fact:

- changed
- moved
- weak
- strong
- hot
- cold
- risk
- blocked
- behind
- important
- valuable
- best
- first

If Antaeus knows the underlying event or measure, name it.

Example:

Instead of `the account moved`, write `the company hired a new COO`.

Instead of `outreach is weak`, write `outreach is your least-completed activity this week` if that is what the product actually means.

---

## 21. Claims discipline

Do not make the copy more precise than the underlying product evidence permits.

If the product knows only that there has been no logged activity, say that. Do not claim that the buyer “lost interest.”

If the product uses public data to infer recruiting capacity, mark it as an inference when appropriate.

If a benchmark is a range, show the range.

Clarity is not license to invent certainty.

---

## 22. Desired voice

The voice should be:

- concise;
- specific;
- technically literate without sounding technical for its own sake;
- calm;
- confident when evidence supports confidence;
- direct;
- willing to say what Antaeus does not do;
- free of enterprise-software filler.

The voice should **not** be:

- cryptic;
- self-consciously profound;
- metaphor-dense;
- cute;
- inflated;
- generic SaaS copy;
- written as if every sentence needs to be quotable.

---

## 23. What not to replace this style with

Clarity does not mean generic enterprise prose.

Reject transformations such as:

> Antaeus is a comprehensive AI-powered revenue enablement platform designed to empower founders and optimize go-to-market outcomes.

That sentence is literal-looking but informationally empty.

Prefer concrete claims with concrete nouns and verbs.

---

## 24. Pre-ship lint

For every visible string, ask:

1. Can a first-time reader understand it without knowing Antaeus vocabulary?
2. Are the important nouns literal?
3. Are the important verbs literal?
4. Does every pronoun have an unmistakable antecedent?
5. If the text gives a recommendation, does it state the evidence or reason?
6. If two facts imply a conclusion, is that relationship explicit?
7. Is a metaphor carrying information that should instead be stated directly?
8. Is software being personified where an actual system behavior can be named?
9. Is a branded term defined on first use?
10. Would removing the cadence reveal that the sentence says very little?
11. Is UI microcopy shorter and more literal than marketing copy?
12. Is the FAQ clearer than the page it explains?
13. Does the wording preserve only claims the product actually supports?
14. Does it pass every rule in §26 (no stand-in nouns, no describing by absence, no judgment words, no app-made threats, no no-exception rules, no "rank")?

If any answer fails, revise before shipping.

---

## 25. Hard target

A page is acceptable when:

- almost all body copy can be interpreted literally;
- proprietary names are few and explicitly defined;
- metaphor is decorative rather than load-bearing;
- no recommendation depends on omitted causal logic;
- product demos use actual states, events, and actions;
- a cold reader can explain Antaeus without borrowing Antaeus's metaphors.

**Target saturation: 5-10/100.**  
Below 5 is possible, but not required if reaching it would erase useful product names or make the writing generic.

---

## 26. Writing to the reader (added 2026-11-09, founder-directed)

These rules came out of the onboarding rewrite. They apply to every visible string.

### Name the thing

Banned patterns, whatever the exact words:

- **Casual stand-in nouns.** An insider-sounding noun used where the specific thing should be named: "the calls," "your read," "the work," "this work." Name it: *who's championing each deal, who signs, what's holding it up.*
- **Describing by absence.** Defining something by where it isn't or who doesn't do it: "what isn't written down anywhere yet," "the questions no CRM asks," "what most setup screens skip." Say what it is.
- **Judgment words about the reader.** Words that assume a failing: "honest," "yet," "really," "actually" (as in "where it actually stands"). Remove the word; the sentence works without it.

### Stakes come from buyers and deals, never from the app

The app does not threaten, catch, name, or report on the user. Pressure comes from what buyers and deals do.

#### Reject

> Any open deal without a next step on the calendar will be named on your Dashboard tomorrow morning.

#### Prefer

> Without that, the first sign one of your deals is in trouble is going to be an email saying they went with someone else.

### No sentences built as rules with no exceptions

Sentences shaped "any X will…," "every Y is…," or "all Z…" read as maxims, even when they describe product behavior. A schedule fact ("every morning") is fine; a universal rule about the reader or their deals is not.

### Write to this reader, about what will happen

Prefer a concrete prediction about the reader's own situation to a general truth. "Is going to be an email" beats "is an email": the first is about the reader, the second could go on a poster. Test every line by asking whether it would make sense on a poster; if it would, rewrite it.

### "Rank" is out

Do not use "rank," "ranks," "ranked," or "ranking" in visible copy. Ordering words ("in order of risk," "sorts," "comes first," "at the top") describe only what the product does to the user's data. They never describe something the user is asked to do or owns.

- Fine: "Your Dashboard lists your open deals in order of risk." On an input screen, a line saying the order those deals will appear in tomorrow is also fine.
- Not fine: "Rank your top accounts," "Put your deals in order of priority," "your ranking."

Code identifiers (`rankByHeat`, `recovery_rank`) are not visible copy and stay.
