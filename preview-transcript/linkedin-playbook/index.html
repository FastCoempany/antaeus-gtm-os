LinkedIn Playbook · tool used during live work · capability map

Plan LinkedIn outreach alongside calls and email.

Room type: a tool used while the work is happening · settled 2026-07-06 (the recommended action)

LinkedIn Playbook recommends an account-specific action from recorded signals, connection requests, messages, and outbound activity. It does not infer trust from activity counts.

Sourced from: canon §4.10 + the recommendation engine (lib/motion.ts) + 5-cue ladder (shipped src/linkedin-playbook). Settled design: linkedin-playbook-one-move-2026-07-06.html.
1 · Purpose

What the room is for

Preserve the five actions and their logging. Recommend the next action from recorded interaction state, and make templates specific to the account. Acceptance and reply statistics describe the sample; they do not establish universal success thresholds.
2 · The five LinkedIn actions

Where you are on the account

#CueWhat it is
1Watchwatch a public signal
2Commentcomment on a specific relevant observation
3Connectreview prior interaction before connecting
4Give-firstgive value before asking
5Askask only when it is warranted
3 · What it offers

The primitives

Capability / surfaceWhat it does
The five LinkedIn actionsThe step ladder showing where you are with the account (above).

The cue-method templatesConnection · Public cue · Give-first · Ask — ready-to-paste scaffolds.
Channel memory statstouches · acceptance · reply rate — the summary of recorded channel outcomes.
The recommended LinkedIn actionThe single next public-first action for the highest-priority account.
4 · Flows

What comes in, what goes out

Flows in
  • account + persona from Signal Console / Outbound Studio
Flows out
  • recorded activity → Dashboard · Signal Console · Readiness · Handoff
5 · Guardrails

Must never be flattened · what it is NOT

Must never be flattened
  • Channel-specific discipline (public cue first, ask last)
  • One recommended LinkedIn action at a time
What it is NOT
  • Never generic outreach tips
  • Not an inbox-first outreach tool
  • Not a connection-request blaster
  • Not a content scheduler
