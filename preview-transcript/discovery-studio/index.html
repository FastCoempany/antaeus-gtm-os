5 · Rails & control

The 7 required rails + the on-call control laws

The 7 rails (always reachable)
Framework rail — the current lens, switchable (not a card gallery)
Segment rail — the ordered list of call segments; one open at a time, jumpable
Recover-the-call rail — authored recover guidance + jump-actions to the segment where the recovery runs
What you have learned (learned-truth ledger) — what the buyer has confirmed so far, and what is still missing
Worked memory — which nodes have been worked (N of essential)
Next-step docket — forces the room toward a real dated next move
Support dossier — supporting evidence, answers to likely objections, and answers to questions the buyer may ask, reachable mid-call
The 3 compression modes
Off — shows all nodes
Essentials — shows only the must-hit nodes for the framework
Emergency — for when the call is going badly: the segment list shrinks to only the segment you are in, and the drawer opens on the recovery suggestions
Mandatory control laws
Skip-ahead handlers · tie-back discipline (holding vs ready to use) · objection + inbound-question handling from the live surface · phase progress & resumption · learned-fact capture close to the live node (never a note graveyard)
The non-negotiable build tests
The seller can: recover from a buyer interruption in one action · switch into compression without losing the next-step path · answer a buyer question without abandoning discovery control · see what-to-say / what-to-listen-for / where-to-go-next without reading paragraphs · resume after being interrupted, challenged, or delayed. And the room always keeps three things separate: facts the buyer confirmed, supporting evidence you hold (case studies, references, data), and what to use now — the point you are ready to say on this call.
