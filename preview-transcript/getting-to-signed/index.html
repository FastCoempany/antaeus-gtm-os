4. What this room is for and how it works

The room's capability map: its purpose, every part and what it does, what flows in from and out to other rooms, and what it must never become.

Getting to Signed · Live Instrument · capability map

The deal isn’t won when they say yes.

Live Instrument (live execution) · renamed from Negotiation 2026-07-07 · settled (face-off + ledger)

Get a verbally-agreed deal actually signed — walk the seller through every front between “we want to buy” and a countersigned contract, push back where they should push back, and keep the buying committee engaged so the deal doesn’t die in procurement. The most common way a B2B deal dies is not losing the argument — it’s internal alignment collapse after the verbal yes.

Sourced from: canon §4.16b (the run-to-signed reconceive + rename, 2026-07-07) + B2B procurement research (SIG/CAIQ · SOC 2/ISO shortcut · liability caps · MAP). Settled design: getting-to-signed-faceoff-2026-07-07.html.
1 · Purpose

What the room is for

A war room for the run to signed, not a rehearsal booth. It leads with the one thing actually blocking right now (the biggest gap between their ask and your line), shown as a face-off — their side ⟷ the gap ⟷ your line — with the seller’s counter, a trade they can offer, and the line they won’t cross. Everything else open sits below as a positions ledger.
2 · The four disciplines

What the room enforces

#DisciplineWhat it means
1Every concession is a deliberate movea give always asks for something back — never a reflex to “be reasonable”
2You already hold the answersa security questionnaire isn’t 150 blank lines — the coverage map shows how many your papers (SOC 2 · ISO 27001 · subprocessor list · pen-test · DPA) already answer
3Keep the committee warma papers-ready strip + a plan-to-signed so nobody goes quiet
4Terms are linked, not a lista liability cap is worthless-or-fine depending on the indemnification ask — linked terms are settled together
3 · The fronts

One ledger row each; the hottest is the face-off

The room morphs which front is the face-off by what’s actually blocking, not a fixed order.
FrontWhat it covers
Legalliability cap + super-cap + indemnification, redlines
Security / InfoSecthe questionnaire + the coverage map + what still needs engineering
Financepayment terms, prepay/discount trades, the signer
Businesskeep the committee moving — champion + economic-buyer engagement, the plan-to-signed

5 · Flows

What comes in, what goes out

Held for the Pro gate: RFP-response handling (formal RFP/RFI intake, question-splitting, drafted answers) — real and wanted, but scoped to a later version, not the first-ship room.
Flows in
  • deal from Deal Workspace (value, stage, decision architecture)
  • the pilot’s evidence from Pilot Desk (leverage in a terms conversation)
  • an advisor from Call in a Favor (a backchannel warming a front)
Flows out
  • front outcomes + the plan-to-signed → Deal Workspace
  • loss-pattern feedback → Future Autopsy (lost in procurement, before terms?)
  • a stuck front → Call in a Favor (route a backchannel to the frozen front)
  • patterns → Founding GTM §6 (why we win) + §4 (where deals leak)
6 · Language — plain, no procurement jargon

The teardown

The seller is a first-timer facing their first enterprise legal/security gauntlet. Security frameworks (SOC 2, ISO 27001, SIG, DPA, pen-test) are named plainly because they’re the real objects — but each is framed as a paper the seller holds (“answers 88 of the questions”), never an acronym to decode.
Banned on the faceSay instead
“counterparty”their side / their legal / their security team
“concession ladder”what you can trade
“redline” (unless taught)the change they want to the contract
the economic buyerthe person who signs off
7 · Guardrails

Must never be flattened · what it is NOT

Must never be flattened
  • The run-to-signed mind (the deal isn’t won at yes)
  • The face-off on the blocking front (their side ⟷ your line, always the one actually blocking)
  • Every-concession-is-a-trade
  • You already hold the answers (the coverage map, never a 150-line data-entry chore)
  • Keeping the committee warm (the plan-to-signed, the engagement reads)
What it is NOT
  • Not a pricing calculator
  • Not a rehearsal booth
  • Never soften into “negotiation tips”
  • RFP tooling is held for the Pro gate — not in the base room
