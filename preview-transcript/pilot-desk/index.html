4. What this room is for and how it works

The room's capability map: its purpose, every part and what it does, what flows in from and out to other rooms, and what it must never become.

Pilot Desk · Decision Bench · capability map

A hand-holding guide to a pilot that actually converts.

Decision Bench (strategic shaping) · renamed from PoC Framework 2026-07-06 · settled (the guided pilot)

A hand-holding pilot guide that walks a first-timer through running a pilot that converts — from setup, through getting the right people using it, to a result the buyer’s boss can act on without you in the room. Not a scorecard; a guide. A pilot is a people operation, not a metric — the success meter is adoption, not whether the outcome number moved yet.

Sourced from: canon §4.15 (the guided-pilot mind expansion + proof/foundry eradication, 2026-07-06) + shipped src/poc-framework. Settled design: pilot-desk-guided-2026-07-06.html.
1 · Purpose

What the room is for

Guide a first-timer through a pilot that gets your hooks in — pull the hands-on users (usually not the buyer or champion), discover hidden stakeholders, multi-thread deeper, and find problems they didn’t know they had. The room is a slow unfolding — one movement at a time, never a wall of fields. It feels customizable; the desk shapes around the deal.
2 · The five movements

Shown one at a time

#MovementWhat it does
1Set it upwhat it has to show · the window · who signs off
2Get the right people inthe hands-on users + champion + who signs off — right-sized (a tight sale needs a small group; the desk sizes the target to the deal)
3Keep the pilot movinghold their hand between check-ins (no rigid weekly clock) — is the product getting used, who else can you pull in
4Read the adoptionare enough of the right people using the product day-to-day
5Write up what it showeda short write-up the champion can take to their boss and defend on their own
3 · What it offers

The primitives

Capability / surfaceWhat it does
The linked dealThe deal the pilot is attached to (from Deal Workspace).
The pilot specwhat it has to show · today’s number · how you’ll measure it · who signs off · when you’ll stop.
The windowVariable, not a fixed 7/14.
The circleA right-sized headcount of who’s hands-on, sized to the deal — headcount lives only here.
Who’s-missing prompts“Someone from IT” / “a day-to-day driver” with an inline find-them → name-them + ask-the-champion-for-an-intro flow.
The adoption meteractually using it (of the people you enrolled, how many are active) + actions taken — fed from the product’s own usage or marked by hand.
Gated per-check-in stepsA step unlocks only when the prior is done.
The Share kitA collapsible library to drive adoption + arm the champion: getting-started video · per-role quick-starts · case study · “why you’re in this pilot” note · champion kit · kickoff announcement · FAQ · reference-customer intro.
The Mutual pilot planYou + the champion agree the goal, steps, and results meeting — co-owned, a shared action, not a solo template.
The write-upThe short document the champion carries to their boss.

5 · Language — the plain-words eradication

No foundry, no “proof”

The old room was a foundry metaphor drenched in banned words — all eradicated on the face. Code enum keys may stay; no user ever sees the banned word.
Banned on the faceSay instead

the quality bandReady / Almost / Too thin to hand over

6 · Guardrails

Must never be flattened · what it is NOT

Must never be flattened
  • The guide (never a scorecard or checklist)
  • Adoption as the success meter (never the outcome number alone)
  • The people / multi-threading layer (right-sized, never “get everyone”)
  • The who’s-missing prompt
  • The feel-of-ease (never a wall of fields)
  • The Share kit + Mutual pilot plan
What it is NOT
  • Never a scorecard
  • Never a checklist
  • Never a wall of fields
  • Not a metrics dashboard
