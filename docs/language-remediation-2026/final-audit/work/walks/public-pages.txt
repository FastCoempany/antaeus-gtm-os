[/why-antaeus/ | at rest | text] CATEGORY · WHAT THIS ACTUALLY IS
[/why-antaeus/ | at rest | heading 1] The operating system for a founder's sales process, built so a first hire can take it over.
[/why-antaeus/ | at rest | text] You already have a CRM. You don't need another sales tool, another AI copilot, or another dashboard. You need a place that records the sales process you currently run from memory — the calls you trust, the deals you push, the pilot results you rely on — so your first serious sales hire starts in a working system instead of inheriting confusion.
[/why-antaeus/ | at rest | text] WHY THIS EXISTS
[/why-antaeus/ | at rest | heading 2] Most founder-led sales processes break at the handoff to the first hire.
[/why-antaeus/ | at rest | text] The founder sells the first $2M from memory. The sales process works because they've been on every call, written every follow-up, and decided every concession. Then they hire a seller — and the hire receives no documented process, no ranked next steps, and no record of why the founder's deals closed. By month three, the founder is running the sales process again, and the new hire is being onboarded for the second time.
[/why-antaeus/ | at rest | text] The pattern is so common it has a category: founder-to-first-operator handoff failure. CRMs don't fix it because CRMs store what gets typed in, not how the founder thinks. AI copilots don't fix it because the copilot has no record of which deals are real. Dashboards don't fix it because they report on the sales process; they don't help anyone run it.
[/why-antaeus/ | at rest | text] Antaeus is built around one decision: the founder's sales process should be a working system the hire can step into. Not an export and not a handoff document — the workspace itself, updated with what the founder did this quarter, ready for the hire to use.
[/why-antaeus/ | at rest | text] CATEGORY · WHAT THIS IS AND ISN'T
[/why-antaeus/ | at rest | heading 2] A system that ranks sales work by risk and evidence.
[/why-antaeus/ | at rest | text] Antaeus is organized around accounts, deals, and signals, not tabs. It ranks risk and urgency across account signals, deals, pilot results, and outreach, and recommends the action that addresses the most risk. Below is what that means in plain language, plus the categories Antaeus is explicitly not.

[/why-antaeus/ | at rest | text] HOW IT WORKS
[/why-antaeus/ | at rest | heading 2] A four-step loop the founder runs and the hire takes over.
[/why-antaeus/ | at rest | text] Each step builds on the one before. An account signal leads to outreach, outreach leads to a deal, the deal produces pilot results, and those results become part of the sales handoff the next seller receives. The loop is the product; each page is where one step gets done.
[/why-antaeus/ | at rest | text] STEP 01
[/why-antaeus/ | at rest | heading 3] Signal
[/why-antaeus/ | at rest | text] A time-limited event suggesting an account may be ready to buy. Ranked by account priority, source, recency, and ICP match, and shown as a ranked list of accounts to act on, not a passive timeline.
[/why-antaeus/ | at rest | text] STEP 02
[/why-antaeus/ | at rest | heading 3] Outreach
[/why-antaeus/ | at rest | text] A specific message, call, or LinkedIn touch with a clear purpose. Written in Outbound Studio, Cold Call Studio, or LinkedIn Playbook — never template spam, always matched to the account, the buyer, their recent engagement, and the trigger.
[/why-antaeus/ | at rest | text] STEP 03
[/why-antaeus/ | at rest | heading 3] Deal + Evidence
[/why-antaeus/ | at rest | text] An opportunity tracked by stage, value, and risk — reviewed in Deal Workspace, checked for loss risk in Future Autopsy, and backed by pilot results the buyer's boss can act on when the stakes rise.
[/why-antaeus/ | at rest | text] STEP 04
[/why-antaeus/ | at rest | heading 3] Handoff
[/why-antaeus/ | at rest | text] Seven written sections the first hire opens on day one: which customers buy and which don't, the outreach that got responses, the questions that won the next meeting, and where deals advance or stall.

[/should-we-hire-an-ae/ | at rest | text] A COMMON HIRING MISTAKE
[/should-we-hire-an-ae/ | at rest | heading 2] A revenue gap doesn’t mean you need another salesperson.
[/should-we-hire-an-ae/ | at rest | text] A company can be short of revenue and still have enough sellers. [/should-we-hire-an-ae/ | at rest | aria-label] “We need another $1.2M” is not the same as “We need another AE.”
[/should-we-hire-an-ae/ | at rest | text] “We need another $1.2M.”
[/should-we-hire-an-ae/ | at rest | text] ≠
[/should-we-hire-an-ae/ | at rest | text] “We need another AE.”
[/should-we-hire-an-ae/ | at rest | text] The analysis looks at the revenue gap and the hiring question separately, so you can see which problem you actually have.
[/should-we-hire-an-ae/ | at rest | text] WHAT WE CHECK
[/should-we-hire-an-ae/ | at rest | heading 2] Another AE is supported only if four things hold up in the numbers.
[/should-we-hire-an-ae/ | at rest | text] 01 — Revenue need Is additional selling capacity actually required? We compare your revenue plan with what your current sellers can realistically close.
[/should-we-hire-an-ae/ | at rest | text] 02 — Pipeline Is there enough qualified pipeline for another seller? We check whether there is enough existing pipeline to hand the new seller, and whether you create new opportunities fast enough to keep them busy.
[/should-we-hire-an-ae/ | at rest | text] 03 — Repeatability Can someone other than the founder close these deals? We separate the deals the founder closed from the deals other sellers closed, and check whether there are enough of the second kind to plan on.
[/should-we-hire-an-ae/ | at rest | text] 04 — Timing Can the hire contribute when the plan needs the revenue? We add up the time it takes to recruit, ramp and close a first deal, and check that someone has time to manage the new hire.
